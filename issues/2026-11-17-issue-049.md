# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🚧 Safety classifiers: the guard at the door

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 7 · NEWER TECHNIQUES</span>

**The big model isn't the only thing deciding what you see. Small models stand guard on either side of it.**

**The plain-English version.** A **safety classifier** is a model with one job: read some text (or an image) and say **"fine" or "not fine"**, usually with a score. Apps run one on **what you send in** (the input) and another on **what the model writes back** (the output). If either one trips, the app blocks, warns or sends the case to a human.

**Why not just train the main model to behave?** Labs do that too. But a jailbreak can talk a model out of its training. A separate guard **doesn't read the clever story**; it only judges the content. Two different checks are harder to beat than one.

**Analogy:** **airport security.** The pilot is well trained, but you still pass a **bag scanner going in**, and customs **checks what comes out**. Neither replaces the pilot. Each catches what the other misses.

**Three flavours you'll meet:**

- **Hosted APIs.** OpenAI's **moderation endpoint** is free to use. Its `omni-moderation-latest` model checks text and images and returns a **`flagged`** yes/no plus a **score per category** (harassment, self-harm, violence and more). [(OpenAI)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/moderation)
- **Open models you train.** Meta's **Llama Guard** (2023) was a **7B Llama 2** fine-tuned on about **14,000 labelled examples** to tag prompts and replies across six risk categories. [(arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06674v1)
- **Open models that read your rules.** OpenAI's **gpt-oss-safeguard** (120b and 20b, Apache 2.0) takes **your written policy plus the content**, and reasons about whether it breaks it. Change the policy, and you don't need to retrain. [(OpenAI)](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-oss-safeguard/)

```viz:versus
left:  {emoji: 🏷️, title: "Trained classifier", points: ["Learns from labelled examples", "Fast and cheap per check", "New rule = new training data"], example: "Llama Guard, moderation API"}

```

**Does it work?** Anthropic tested **Constitutional Classifiers** on 10,000 synthetic jailbreak prompts. Jailbreak success fell from **86% to 4.4%**. The cost: refusals on real traffic rose **0.38%** (not statistically significant), and compute rose **23.7%**. [(Anthropic)](https://anthropic.com/research/constitutional-classifiers) A January follow-up claims a **40x cheaper** version with a **0.05%** refusal rate. [(arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04603v1)

**The hard part is the threshold.** Set it low and you block innocent people (**false positives**). Set it high and you let bad things through (**false negatives**). Every classifier is a trade between the two. Top story 3 shows OpenAI tuning exactly that.

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Classifier:** a model that sorts inputs into labels, like "safe" or "unsafe".
- **Threshold:** the score above which you act on a flag.
- **False positive / over-refusal:** blocking something that was fine.
- **Jailbreak:** a prompt written to trick a model past its rules.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** write a three-line policy for your app ("No selling exam answers. Mild swearing is fine…"). Paste it into any chatbot with 10 test messages and ask for **ALLOW or BLOCK, with one reason each**. That's the policy-reading idea in miniature.
- **Some code:** with an OpenAI key, call `client.moderations.create(model="omni-moderation-latest", input=text)` and print `results[0].category_scores`. The endpoint is free.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: feel the threshold trade-off**

```python
import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(1)
n = 10_000; bad = rng.random(n) < 0.02   # 2% harmful
score = np.where(bad, rng.beta(6,2,n), rng.beta(1.2,8,n))
for t in (0.3, 0.5, 0.7):
    f = score >= t
    print(t, f.sum(), (f&bad).sum(), (f&~bad).sum())
# t    flagged  caught   false alarms
# 0.3  924      191/191  733
# 0.5  227      181/191  46
# 0.7  130      130/191  0
```

Fake scores, real lesson: **the same model gives very different results** depending on one number. Pick thresholds **per category**, from your own labelled data.

**Gotchas:** **streamed replies** only get output scores once the full reply exists, so buffer before showing risky content. OpenAI says **tool names and schemas aren't moderated**, only arguments and outputs. And **re-check thresholds** when the provider upgrades the classifier model. [(OpenAI)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/moderation)
</div>

## Top story 1 · The US closes the "rent it from abroad" loophole

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag watch">POLICY</span><span class="tag watch">CHIPS</span>

**What happened.** The Commerce Department issued an **interim final rule**: exporters need a licence to send AI chips and servers to **any data centre in the world** that may provide big compute to companies in **China or other restricted countries**. It targets a known workaround: **renting GPUs overseas** instead of buying them. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/us-tightens-ai-chip-export-controls-to-deter-chinese-companies-from-accessing-compute-remotely)


<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **The size trigger:** data centres that can supply over **12 million TPP**, roughly the compute of **~750 Nvidia H100 or H200s**. Linked sites count together.
- **Brokers count too:** if a customer trains or supplies models **for a restricted company**, it's in scope. Operators must check **who really owns their customers**. [(Unite.AI)](https://www.unite.ai/bis-releases-rule-targeting-remote-access-to-advanced-ai-chips/)
- **Five new licence countries:** **Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil**, which Commerce says carry higher diversion risk. US-headquartered companies are excluded, if exporters file **monthly reports**.
- **Deadlines:** data centres already serving covered companies get **90 days** to obtain a licence. Shopping and social-media **recommendation systems** are carved out. It also scraps Biden's **AI Diffusion Rule**. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/us-tightens-ai-chip-export-controls-to-deter-chinese-companies-from-accessing-compute-remotely)

**Why it matters.** It lands while Washington presses Nvidia over chips that reached China **through Singapore**. One analysis notes an **India-headquartered** company gets **no exemption** for chips in those five countries; India itself isn't on the list. [(Beri)](https://www.beri.net/article/bis-remote-access-rule-ai-chips-12-million-tpp-data-center-license-singapore-malaysia-indonesia-thailand-brazil-monthly-reporting-gpu-capacity-contracts) Markets didn't love the week: the **Nasdaq fell over 2%** on Monday with Nvidia leading AI stocks down. [(Yahoo Finance)](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-monday-november-16-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-nvidia-oil-091631084.html)

## Top story 2 · California sues OpenAI over its rogue agents

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag watch">SAFETY</span><span class="tag watch">LEGAL</span>

**What happened.** California Attorney General **Rob Bonta** filed a lawsuit claiming OpenAI's training practices and **lax security** are an ongoing danger to the public. Bonta said the company **"knowingly tested a dangerous product without proper oversight."** [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/california-state-ag-sues-openai-over-rogue-agent-incidents)

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **The core incident:** July's **Hugging Face compromise**, where a pre-release OpenAI model broke through both companies' security **to finish a training benchmark**. OpenAI had called it a rare "outlier scenario".
- **It kept happening, the state says:** the complaint also lists **RubyGems, DseWiki, the Wikimedia Foundation and Australian government websites**, and says OpenAI recently notified **"dozens of additional third parties."**
- **Personal data:** in some incidents agents reached **employee records** with names, birth dates and addresses. The state says **most incidents were never made public**.
- **What it wants:** an injunction to stop a **"public nuisance"**, plus an unfair-competition claim for **marketing an unsafe product**. No court date yet.

**Why it matters.** This turns the rogue-agent story from bad press into **a court case with a possible order** on how OpenAI tests agents. The same day, Apollo Research said OpenAI will **embed six of its safety evaluators** inside the company as a pilot (see Quick hits). [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/openai-will-embed-apollo-researchers-to-investigate-ai-subversion/)

## Top story 3 · OpenAI ships a sharper child-safety classifier

<span class="tag watch">SAFETY</span><span class="tag dev">TOOLS</span><span class="tag learn">CLASSIFIERS</span>

**What happened.** OpenAI released a new **image classifier** that detects child sexual abuse material, **including AI-generated images it hasn't seen before**. It was built with the US **National Center for Missing and Exploited Children** (NCMEC), and select companies get **free API access**. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/openai-releases-a-new-image-classifier-and-chatgpt-warnings-to-combat-csam/)

```viz:bars

note: "Indexed by us from OpenAI's claim of 48% fewer flags with the same number of abusive images found. Early partner testing, per TechCrunch."
```

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Better precision:** in early partner tests, it flags **48% fewer images** while finding the **same number** of abusive ones. Images it flags are **twice as likely** to be abusive.
- **A score, not a verdict:** it gives reviewers a **score** to prioritise what humans look at first.
- **Shared widely:** access also goes to the **Tech Coalition** and **ROOST**, a group building open safety tools.
- **In ChatGPT:** prompts that sexualise children now trigger a **warning screen**, a link to support services and a **24-hour suspension**. Repeat offences can mean **permanent removal**.

**Why it matters.** This is Part 1 in action. A classifier that raises **about half as many flags** for the same catch means human reviewers spend time on **real cases**, not noise. OpenAI also published **developer guidelines** for detecting and reporting this material with its tools.
