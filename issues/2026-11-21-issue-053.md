# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🌍 World models: AI that imagines a world you can explore

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 7 · NEWER TECHNIQUES</span>

**A video model makes you a clip. A world model makes you a place, and keeps drawing it as you move.**

**The plain-English version.** A **text-to-video model** takes a prompt and makes a fixed clip; you watch it, and that's it. A **world model** generates the scene **one frame at a time, in response to what you do**. Press "forward" and it draws what you'd see one step ahead. Turn left and it draws the left. Nothing is pre-built: there's **no game engine, no 3D map**, just a model predicting the next frame.

**Analogy:** close your eyes and imagine walking from your bedroom to the kitchen. Your brain **predicts what you'd see at each step**: the door, the fridge, the steel dabba on the counter. That inner simulator is a world model. Now imagine someone shouting "it's raining inside!" and your picture changes. That's a **promptable world event**.


**The key word is autoregressive.** Each new frame depends on the frames before it, the way each word an LLM writes depends on the words before. DeepMind says Genie 3 has to take the **whole trajectory so far** into account, with visual memory reaching back **about a minute**. It ran at **720p and 24 fps** and stayed consistent for **a few minutes**. [(Google DeepMind)](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/)

**The weak spot is memory.** Look away too long and the model has to **re-imagine** what was there, and it may imagine something different. Developers testing Genie 4 report that looking away for **8+ seconds**, or spinning 360°, can make doors and landmarks **shift or reset**, and that signs and clocks drift. [(PPC Land)](https://ppc.land/google-charges-developers-about-10-cents-a-second-for-genie-4-worlds/)

**Why do labs care?** Not just for games. A world model is a **cheap, safe simulator**. You can train a robot or an AI agent in an imagined warehouse instead of a real one. The idea is old: in 2018, David Ha and Jürgen Schmidhuber trained an agent **entirely inside its own "dream"** of a game, then moved it back to the real game. [(arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122v1) NVIDIA's **Cosmos** platform does this for robots and cars: its Predict models forecast the future of a scene **as video**. [(NVIDIA)](https://docs.nvidia.com/cosmos/latest/introduction.html)

**Why now?** On Thursday Google opened **Genie 4** to developers through the Gemini API (story 1 below). For the first time, a frontier world model is something you can **call from code**.

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **World model:** an AI that predicts what happens next in an environment, given an action.
- **Autoregressive:** each output (frame or word) is built from the ones before it.
- **Object permanence:** things staying put when you look away. World models still struggle here.
- **Sim-to-real:** training in a simulated world, then using the skill in the real one.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** in any video tool you have, prompt a slow camera that **pans away from a red scooter and back again**. Is it still the same scooter, in the same spot? That's the object-permanence test.
- **Some code:** run the toy below. Shrink `memory` and watch the street **rewrite itself** behind you.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: why things change when you turn round, in 14 lines**

```python
import random; random.seed(7)
THINGS = ["tree", "house", "cow", "bike"]
street = [random.choice(THINGS) for _ in range(12)]  # pass 12 things, one per frame
def turn_back(memory):            # frames the model can still "see"
    changed = 0
    for i, real in enumerate(street):
        age = 12 - i              # how many frames ago you passed it
        redrawn = real if age <= memory else random.choice(THINGS)
        changed += redrawn != real
    return changed
for memory in (16, 8, 4):
    avg = sum(turn_back(memory) for _ in range(1000)) / 1000
    print(f"memory {memory:>2} frames: {avg:.1f} of 12 things changed")
# memory 16: 0.0 | memory 8: 3.0 | memory 4: 6.0
```

The lesson: anything **older than the model's memory** gets redrawn from scratch. Real models are far smarter than a random guess, but the trade-off is the same: **longer memory costs more compute** per frame.

**Gotchas (Genie 4 API):** it uses the **Interactions API, not the Live API**. Use WebRTC in the browser and WebSocket on a server, with **single-session ephemeral tokens** so your key never reaches the client. **End sessions explicitly** to stop billing, and plan for **5–9 seconds** before the first frame. [(PPC Land)](https://ppc.land/google-charges-developers-about-10-cents-a-second-for-genie-4-worlds/)
</div>

## Top story 1 · Update: Genie 4 opens to developers, at about 10 cents a second

<span class="tag dev">DEV</span><span class="tag try">TRY IT</span><span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span>

**What happened.** Google made **Genie 4** available to developers through the **Gemini API** and Google AI Studio. You start a world with a text prompt, an image or both, then walk it with the keyboard and change it with **live text prompts** (weather, lighting, new objects). It streams **720p at 24 fps with native audio**, and every output carries an invisible **SynthID watermark**. [(Google)](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/genie-4-in-gemini-api/) We first covered Genie 4's announcement in Issue #29.

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Model ID:** `genie-4`. It needs a **paid-tier** API key; no free tier is described. [(TestingCatalog)](https://www.testingcatalog.com/google-opens-genie-4-world-model-to-developers-in-gemini-api/)
- **The bill:** each frame counts as **240 video output tokens**, so one second is **5,760 tokens**. Audio is bundled in. [(PPC Land)](https://ppc.land/google-charges-developers-about-10-cents-a-second-for-genie-4-worlds/)
- **The limits:** sessions stop at **120 seconds**, can't be resumed, and may get less stable after **60 to 90 seconds**. Text steering takes **1–2 seconds** to show up.
- **Not supported:** batch mode, function calling, Search grounding, portrait video. Prompts with **real people or brand IP** can be blocked, or can end a running stream.
- **Where:** PPC Land lists serving regions as the **US, Western Europe and Japan**. India isn't mentioned in any source we saw.

**Why it matters.** World models move from **demo videos to a metered API**. At about **$360 an hour** by PPC Land's maths, it's a prototyping tool, not a game engine, for now.

## Top story 2 · AWS lets AI agents shop, and puts the bill on your AWS account

<span class="tag dev">DEV</span><span class="tag watch">AGENTS</span>

**What happened.** AWS Marketplace now lets agents **find and buy select products per request**, such as a single web search or a call to a paid guardrail service. Payment uses the open **x402** protocol through **AgentCore payments**, settles in US dollars on your **existing AWS bill**, and needs **no crypto wallet**. [(AWS)](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/marketplace-agentic-payments-agentcore/)


<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Your guardrails first:** you set **spending limits, approved sellers and rules** before an agent can transact. Every purchase lands in **CloudTrail** for audit. [(AWS)](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/awsmarketplace/aws-marketplace-helps-humans-and-agents-buy-deploy-and-transact/)
- **For sellers:** you register with Marketplace and add AWS's **x402 SDK** to your listing; AWS collects the money and pays you as usual.
- **Also new:** an agent-mode checkout that still needs **human confirmation**, a deployment agent that shows an estimated monthly cost first, and approvals over email for people **without an AWS account**.
- **The numbers:** AWS's Matt Yanchyshyn told Constellation that Agent Mode buyers convert at **6% to 7%**. A trust agent covers **4,500 of about 35,000 listings** at launch. [(Constellation Research)](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/aws-marketplace-adds-bevy-agentic-commerce-agents-removes-approval-friction-purchases)

**Why it matters.** Agents that **pay for their own tools** could change how APIs are sold: per call, with no sign-up page. It also means **your budget settings are now a safety feature**.
