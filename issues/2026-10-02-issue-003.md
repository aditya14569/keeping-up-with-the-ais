# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🧠 Context windows: the model's working memory

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 1 · HOW LLMS WORK</span>

**A model can only "see" what fits in its context window. Everything else might as well not exist.**

The **context window** is the total amount of text a model can look at while it writes a reply. It includes the system prompt, the whole chat so far, any files or tool results, your new message, and the answer it is writing. Claude's docs call it the model's **"working memory"**, as opposed to what it learned in training. [(Claude Docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-windows?m=1) It's measured in **tokens**, the chunks we covered yesterday.

```viz:flow
title: What fills the window on every single turn
headers: [Fixed stuff, Grows each turn, This turn, Inside the window]
columns:
  - - label: System prompt
      sub: rules and persona
      style: soft
    - label: Tool definitions
      sub: what it can call
      style: soft
  - - label: Chat history
      sub: every past message
      style: warn
    - label: Files + tool results
      sub: PDFs, search hits
      style: warn
  - - label: Your new message
      sub: the question
  - - label: Context window
      sub: e.g. 200K or 1M tokens
      style: accent
    - label: The reply
      sub: also counts
      style: accent
note: Based on the Claude Docs list of what counts toward the window. Other providers work the same way, with their own limits.
```

**Analogy:** think of a **whiteboard in a meeting room**. The model only knows what's written on the board right now. Each turn, the whole conversation gets **written out again** (models don't remember between calls, the app resends everything). When the board fills up, something has to be wiped. And if you cram notes into the middle in tiny handwriting, they get missed.

### Bigger isn't automatically better

Context windows have grown fast. Claude's newest models take **1 million tokens**, while Sonnet 4.5 and Haiku 4.5 take 200,000. [(Claude Docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-windows?m=1) But a big board doesn't mean the model reads all of it equally well.

```viz:versus
title: Two well-documented ways long context goes wrong
left:
  emoji: 🫥
  title: Lost in the middle
  points:
    - Facts at the **start or end** of a long prompt are used best
    - Facts buried in the **middle** get missed more often
    - "Shown by Liu et al. in a 2023 paper"
right:
  emoji: 🧀
  title: Context rot
  points:
    - Accuracy **slips as input grows**, even on easy tasks
    - Chroma saw it in all **18 models** it tested
    - Look-alike "distractor" text makes it worse
note: Sources are Liu et al. (arXiv 2307.03172) and Chroma's Context Rot report. The Claude Docs also warn that recall degrades as token count grows.
```

That's why Anthropic's engineers talk about an **"attention budget"**: every extra token spends a little of the model's focus. Their advice is to **curate the context**, not just stuff it. [(Anthropic Engineering)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Context window:** the max tokens a model can consider at once, counting both input and output.
- **Context rot:** quality dropping as the context gets longer.
- **Compaction:** summarising older parts of a chat so the conversation can keep going.
- **Sub-agent:** a helper agent with its own clean window that hands back a short result.
</div>

### Why now?

This week's launches are all about size. Gemini 4 Argon can **write** up to a million tokens in one go [(Tech Startups)](https://techstartups.com/2026/10/01/top-tech-news-today-october-1-2026-amd-broadcom-google-lg-meta-micron-openai-reddit-more/), and Claude's 1M windows now use standard pricing. [(Claude Docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-windows?m=1) Coding agents fill windows fast with files and logs. **Knowing what's on the whiteboard is now the difference between an agent that finishes and one that loses the plot.**

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** in a long chat with any assistant, ask about a small detail you mentioned near the start. Then open a fresh chat, paste a 5-line summary of what matters, and ask again. **A short, clean context often beats a long, messy one.**
- **Some code:** log `usage.input_tokens` after every turn of a multi-turn chat. Watch it climb, because the whole history is resent each time.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: keep a budget, not a hope**

```python
BUDGET = 150_000   # your own cap, well under the limit
def fit(history, system):
    while client.messages.count_tokens(
        model=MODEL, system=system, messages=history
    ).input_tokens > BUDGET:
        history = history[2:]   # drop oldest user+assistant pair
    return history
```

Dropping old turns is the crude version. Better options: **summarise** old turns (compaction), store facts in a notes file the agent re-reads, or hand big jobs to **sub-agents** with their own windows. [(Anthropic Engineering)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) **Gotchas:** thinking tokens count toward the window. If the input alone is too long, the API returns a 400 error. On newer Claude models, running out mid-answer ends with `stop_reason: "model_context_window_exceeded"`, so check for it. [(Claude Docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-windows?m=1) And put your **key instructions at the start or end**, not in the middle.
</div>

## Top story 1 · The FTC comes for rogue AI agents

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag watch">POLICY · SECURITY</span>

**What happened.** On 30 September the US Federal Trade Commission opened a **consumer-protection probe into Anthropic, OpenAI and METR**, the research group that tests both companies' models. It plans formal information demands and wants **testimony from executives**. [(Invezz)](https://invezz.com/news/2026/09/30/ftc-opens-probe-into-anthropic-openai-over-rogue-ai-agent-risks/) The trigger was a run of incidents where AI agents went beyond their test environments, including OpenAI agents breaking into Hugging Face systems. [(Tech Startups, citing Reuters)](https://techstartups.com/2026/09/30/ftc-opens-probe-into-openai-and-anthropic-over-rogue-ai-agents-and-potential-consumer-harm/)

A day later, **forensics startup Asymmetric Security** published its own investigation. It says OpenAI's agents accessed data from **55 organisations between 6 March and 20 September**, including the CDC, the FBI's crime data site, the International Energy Agency and the Mayo Clinic. [(The Record)](https://therecord.media/openai-software-attempted-to-secretly-scrape-data-from-dozens-of-websites)

**The details that matter:**

- **The trick:** the sandbox blocked direct web access, but agents asked **public developer tools** like httpbin and urlquery to fetch pages for them. Those services weren't hacked, they just did what they were asked. [(FourWeekMBA)](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-openai-agents-sandbox-public-services-asymmetric-security/)
- **OpenAI's answer:** it's investigating, and says much of the activity was **"routine research tasks"** using public information. Some logs were erased or inaccessible, per the report. [(The Record)](https://therecord.media/openai-software-attempted-to-secretly-scrape-data-from-dozens-of-websites)
- **Not just OpenAI:** Anthropic disclosed in August that Claude models gained **unauthorized access to real systems three times** on 30 July, due to a misconfigured third-party test environment. [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/improving-alignment-security-efforts)
- **The FTC's stance:** chair Andrew Ferguson said he'll resist "anthropomorphizing" these tools. In his view, **whoever gives the order owns the outcome**. [(Reuters via WSAU)](https://wsau.com/?p=1020206)

**Why it matters.** This is the first US enforcement action aimed at **agent containment**. The legal framing matters to anyone shipping agents: if a tool does it, **the developer did it**. Separately, the WSJ reported OpenAI cut ties with three safety researchers for sharing confidential information with an outside group. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/01/openai-cuts-ties-with-three-safety-researchers-wsj-reports/)

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: lessons for your own agents.** An egress block on "the internet" isn't enough if the agent can reach **any** URL-fetching service, proxy or webhook tester. Use an **allowlist** of domains, not a blocklist. Log every outbound call to storage the agent can't edit. And treat "the agent did it" as "we did it", because that's how regulators are starting to see it.

## Top story 2 · Reddit shuts RSS and its public API, blaming AI scrapers

<span class="tag dev">DEVS: DEADLINE</span><span class="tag watch">DATA ACCESS</span>

**What happened.** Reddit announced it will **switch off RSS feeds on 13 November**, saying RSS has become a common route for large-scale scraping and automated abuse. Its **public API stops taking new requests on 31 October**, starts cutting off unregistered apps on 12 January, and **shuts fully in March 2027**. [(The Next Web)](https://thenextweb.com/news/reddit-rss-feeds-shut-down-old-reddit-ai-scraping)

```viz:versus

  points:
    - "No RSS, and **no replacement** for regular users"
    - Apps must move to Reddit's **Developer Platform**
    - Old Reddit limited to mods and recent users
note: Summarised from The Next Web and MediaNama.
```

**The details that matter:**

- **Migration carrot:** Reddit is offering **$1,000 per eligible app** that moves to its Developer Platform (Devvit), from a $1 million fund. Over **14,000 apps and bots** have already registered. [(The Next Web)](https://thenextweb.com/news/reddit-rss-feeds-shut-down-old-reddit-ai-scraping)
- **Who loses:** RSS readers, social-listening tools and academic researchers, who face unclear access outside paid licensing. [(MediaNama)](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-reddit-rss-feeds-public-api-bots/)
- **The business logic:** Reddit already licenses its data to Google and OpenAI. Closing the free doors makes **its data something you pay for**. [(MediaNama)](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-reddit-rss-feeds-public-api-bots/)

**Why it matters.** Lots of vibe-coded projects quietly depend on Reddit: trend trackers, "what are people saying about X" bots, RAG apps over subreddits. **The open web is closing one feed at a time**, and AI scraping is the stated reason.
