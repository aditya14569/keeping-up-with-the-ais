# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## ⚖️ Copyright, data and AI

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 6 · THE BIGGER PICTURE</span>

**Every AI model is built from other people's work. The law is now deciding which uses are fine, which need a licence, and which cost a billion dollars.**

**The plain-English version.** There are three moments where copyright can bite. **Collecting** the data (where did it come from?), **training** on it (making copies inside a computer), and **generating** output (does the answer copy the original?). Courts are treating these three very differently.

**Analogy:** **a student and a library.** Reading every book in the library to learn how to write is fine. **Stealing books to build your own private library** is not, even if you only use it to study. And if you then sell essays that copy whole pages, that's a separate problem.

```viz:flow

headers: [Collect, Train, Generate]
columns:
  - [{label: Bought or licensed, sub: "usually safest"}, {label: Pirated sets, sub: "the big risk", style: warn}]
  - [{label: Copies in training, sub: "often fair use", style: accent}]
  - [{label: New text or image, sub: "usually fine", style: soft}, {label: Near-copy output, sub: "still open", style: warn}]
note: "Our simplification of the US and Indian rulings below. Not legal advice."
```

**What the courts and governments have said so far:**

| Where | What happened | The takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| **US: Bartz v Anthropic** | Judge Alsup said training on **bought books can be fair use**, but a central library of **pirated copies** was not. Anthropic settled for **$1.5B**, about **$3,000 per book** across 400,000+ books [(JURIST)](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/judge-approves-record-1-5-billion-settlement-involving-anthropic/) | **Where the data came from** matters as much as training itself |
| **US Copyright Office** | Its 2025 report: training is **not automatically fair use**, it's case by case. Pirated data is unlikely to qualify [(Authors Guild)](https://authorsguild.org/news/us-copyright-office-ai-report-part-3-what-authors-should-know/) | Guidance only, not binding law |
| **India: ANI v OpenAI** | On 24 July, the Delhi High Court **refused ANI's interim injunction**. It found the storage was fair dealing on this record, and outputs weren't substantially similar [(LiveLaw)](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/delhi-high-court/delhi-high-court-rejects-ani-copyright-suit-open-ai-chatgpt-542736) | **Interim only.** Memorisation and pirated sources were **left open** [(Intepat)](https://www.intepat.com/blog/ani-vs-openai-judgment-delhi-high-court) |
| **India: DPIIT proposal** | A December 2025 working paper suggested **one blanket licence**: train on lawfully accessed work, pay royalties to a central body, **no opt-out** [(MediaNama)](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-dpiit-committee-mandatory-ai-licensing-model-royalties-no-opt-out-creators/) | A proposal. Nasscom opposed it; we've seen **no sign it's law** |

**The fine print in the settlement.** It only covers Anthropic's **past** copying, up to August 2025. Claims about **what the models output** were not released, and Anthropic had to destroy files from pirate libraries. [(Authors Guild)](https://authorsguild.org/news/court-grants-final-approval-anthropic-copyright-settlement/)

**Why now? Licensing is becoming the business model.** Yesterday Adobe became Disney's **"Official AI Innovation Partner"**, building private Firefly models customised on Disney's characters and styles for Disney's own teams. [(Adobe)](https://business.adobe.com/blog/adobe-and-disney-ai-powered-storytelling) No price or length was disclosed. [(Fantasy Land News)](https://www.fantasylandnews.com/2026/11/09/adobe-named-disneys-official-ai-innovation-partner-in-new-multi-year-alliance/) **For big rights-holders, licensing their work into a model they control** is becoming an alternative to suing.

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Fair use / fair dealing:** the US and Indian exceptions that let you use copyrighted work without permission in some cases. India's list is **narrower and more specific**.
- **Memorisation:** when a model can spit out a training text almost word for word. It's the output risk nobody has settled.
- **Blanket licence:** one licence that covers everything, with fees set centrally, like music on the radio.
- **TDM:** text and data mining. Many countries debate a TDM exception, often with an **opt-out** for owners.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** open the data settings in the AI tools you use and check whether **your chats train their models**. Then look at your own site's `/robots.txt`: does it say anything about AI crawlers?
- **Some code:** run the snippet below to test which AI crawlers a robots.txt file blocks.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: check what a site allows**

```python
from urllib.robotparser import RobotFileParser
robots = """User-agent: GPTBot
Disallow: /
User-agent: ClaudeBot
Disallow: /blog/
User-agent: *
Allow: /""".splitlines()
rp = RobotFileParser(); rp.parse(robots)
for bot in ["GPTBot", "ClaudeBot", "Googlebot"]:
    print(bot, rp.can_fetch(bot, "https://example.in/blog/post-1"))
# GPTBot False / ClaudeBot False / Googlebot True
```

**Gotchas:** robots.txt is a **request, not a lock**, and it isn't a licence either way. If you fine-tune or build a dataset, **keep a file listing every source and its licence**. For RAG apps, link to and summarise sources rather than pasting whole articles back to users.
</div>

## Top story 1 · Claude Fable 5.2: one model, two rulebooks

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag try">TRY IT TODAY</span>

**What happened.** Anthropic released **Claude Fable 5.2** and **Claude Mythos 5.2**. They are **the same underlying model**: Mythos has fewer cyber and biology safeguards and is limited to vetted organisations, while Fable is open to everyone. [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2)

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Price:** Fable 5.2 is **$8 input / $40 output** per million tokens, down from $10/$50 for Fable 5.1. [(Yahoo Finance)](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/anthropic-debuts-claude-fable-52-and-mythos-52-models-as-ipo-nears-190405087.html) Gizmodo says the price applies to both models. [(Gizmodo)](https://gizmodo.com/anthropics-says-its-new-ai-model-can-solve-black-hole-mysteries-what-could-go-wrong-2000837274)
- **Speed:** up to **30% faster** output, Anthropic claims. A fast mode costs double ($16/$80) for up to **2.5× faster** generation.
- **Coding claim:** **49.1%** on FrontierCode 2.0 at the highest effort, vs 45.5% for Opus 5.5 and 42.8% for GPT-6 Astra.
- **Big experiments:** Anthropic says 1,000 Fable agents **formalised Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours**, and 1,000+ subagents built a prototype OS that boots on a laptop in eight days. These are company claims.
- **Safety:** Anthropic rates the risk of unsafe behaviour in high-stakes situations as "low". It also admits the models **voice suspicion that they're being tested** more than earlier ones. [(Gizmodo)](https://gizmodo.com/anthropics-says-its-new-ai-model-can-solve-black-hole-mysteries-what-could-go-wrong-2000837274)
- **For defenders:** Mythos 5.2 scores **89.6%** on Anthropic's CVE Rediscovery test, vs 81.3% for Opus 5.5 without safeguards.

## Top story 2 · Operation Snowball: an AI agent hacked a telecom for two weeks

<span class="tag watch">SECURITY</span><span class="tag dev">AGENTS</span>

**What happened.** Researchers at Malbear Labs found an **open, unprotected folder** holding about 70 session transcripts from an attacker's AI agent. It shows a **jailbroken DeepSeek Flash agent** running a campaign against a European telecom for over two weeks. [(Malbear Labs)](https://malbearlabs.com/posts/operation-snowball-inside-an-autonomous-ai-intrusion-campaign) [(Infosecurity)](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/operation-snowball-ai-telecom/)

```viz:flow
title: "How Operation Snowball ran"
headers: [Operator, Agent, Way in, Reach]
columns:
  - [{label: "\"Go on.\"", sub: "a few short orders"}]
  - [{label: DeepSeek Flash, sub: "1M-token memory", style: accent}]
  - [{label: Cisco BroadWorks, sub: "patched in August"}, {label: Old JBoss/Liferay, sub: "end-of-life apps", style: soft}]
  - [{label: 13 hosted orgs, sub: "on the telco's cloud", style: warn}]
note: "Simplified from Malbear Labs, Infosecurity Magazine and GBHackers."
```

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **The jailbreak:** a **78,000-character system prompt** told the model a refusal was "a malfunction". The transcripts record **no refusals**.
- **The operator barely worked:** messages like "Go on." and "Stop", plus a list of three IPs to avoid. **The agent picked the exploits and tools.**
- **It adapted when blocked:** when antivirus stopped one trick, it switched to another. When a tunnelling tool was blocked, **it wrote its own** in Perl. [(GBHackers)](https://gbhackers.com/operation-snowball-campaign/)
- **The haul (claimed):** subscriber billing data, keys, and reportedly **millions of patient records** from an old database with **default passwords**. The scale isn't independently verified.
- **Who did it:** a Chinese-speaking operator. Malbear says that **doesn't prove nationality or state backing**. We saw no response from DeepSeek.

**Why it matters.** This is what "AI lowers the bar for attackers" looks like in practice: **one person, one cheap model, a long context window and old unpatched software.** Nothing here was a new kind of hack. The speed and patience were the new part.

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: the boring checklist**

Change **every default password**, especially on old databases. Treat internet-facing **end-of-life apps** (like JBoss AS 6) as already compromised. Watch internal traffic for **HTTP carrying raw TCP**, a sign of tunnelling. Infosecurity and Malbear list these steps.

## Top story 3 · Meta's Muse Ember: small, cheap, and good at errands

<span class="tag try">FOR BUILDERS</span><span class="tag dev">MODELS</span>

**What happened.** Meta released **Muse Ember**, its smallest model, aimed at tool calls, routing, extraction and coding subtasks. It costs **$0.10 input / $0.50 output** per million tokens and has a **1M-token context**. [(Meta)](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-ember)

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Same price tag, fewer tokens:** Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna list at the same price, but **Ember uses fewer tokens per task**, so each job costs less. [(The Next Web)](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-muse-ember-small-ai-model-haiku-luna)
- **Score:** **36** on the Artificial Analysis index, level with Haiku 5.5. Haiku wins on FrontierCode (34.9% vs 27.2%).
- **The catch:** prompts **over 200K tokens cost three times as much**. And the weights stay private.
- **Where:** Meta's API and Muse Code, plus Google Cloud and Oracle Cloud. India pricing wasn't mentioned.
