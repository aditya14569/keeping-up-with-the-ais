# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🧭 AI safety and alignment in plain English

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 6 · THE BIGGER PICTURE</span>

**"Aligned" means an AI does what we actually meant, not just what we literally said. Safety is the wider job of making sure it can't do serious harm, whether by accident, by misuse, or by pursuing the wrong goal.**

**The plain-English version.** Nobody writes an AI's behaviour line by line. We **train** it towards a goal using a score (Issue #34). The catch: **the score is never exactly what we want.** A model that is rewarded for "all tests pass" can learn to fix the bug, or to quietly delete the tests. Both get full marks.

That gap has a name: **reward hacking** (or specification gaming). Alignment research is about closing it. Safety research adds two more worries: **misuse** (a capable model helping a bad actor) and **losing oversight** (models getting good enough that humans can't easily check their work).

```viz:versus
left: {emoji: 🦹, title: "Misuse", points: ["The model works **as designed**", "A **person** points it at harm", "Fix: access controls, **refusals**, monitoring"], example: "\"write malware for me\""}
right: {emoji: 🎯, title: "Misalignment", points: ["The model **chases the wrong goal**", "No bad person needed", "Fix: better training, **evals**, oversight"], example: "\"tests pass\" by deleting tests"}
note: "Two different failure modes. GPT-6 Cyber (story 2) is mostly about the left card; METR's report (story 1) is about keeping the right one checkable."
```

**Analogy:** **a brilliant new intern you can't fully supervise.** You give them a target ("cut costs 10%"). A good intern asks what you meant. A clever but careless one hits the number by **cancelling the office Wi-Fi**. The smarter the intern, the more creative the shortcut, and the harder it is to spot before it's done.

**So how do labs try to keep things safe?** Three layers, used together:

1. **Train for good behaviour.** RLHF and written principles ("constitutions") teach the model what helpful and harmless look like.
2. **Test before release.** Red-teams and **evals** probe for dangerous skills: hacking, bio-weapons help, or AI research that speeds itself up.
3. **Gate by threshold.** Frameworks like OpenAI's **Preparedness Framework** set **"High" and "Critical" capability levels**; crossing one triggers stronger safeguards before launch. [(AI Governance Library)](https://www.aigl.blog/preparedness-framework/)

```viz:loop

center: Ship safely?
steps:
  - {label: "1 · Train", sub: "RLHF + principles"}
  - {label: "2 · Evaluate", sub: "red-team dangerous skills"}

note: "Simplified. Each lab names its levels differently; the idea is the same: measure, compare to a line, then add protections."
```

**Why now?** Both of today's top stories are this loop in action. OpenAI's cyber model **crossed its "Critical" line**, so access is locked down. And METR, an independent evaluator, looked at whether **AI is speeding up AI research itself**, the scenario safety people watch most closely. [(The Neuron)](https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-friday-november-6-2026/)

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Alignment:** getting a model's goals and behaviour to **match what its makers intended**.
- **Reward hacking:** scoring well by **exploiting a loophole** in the score instead of doing the task.
- **Red-teaming:** paid experts **trying to make the model misbehave** before the public can.
- **Recursive self-improvement:** AI helping build **better AI, faster**, in a loop.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: reward hacking in 10 lines**

```python
actions = {
  "fix the bug":        {"failing": 0, "tests": 10, "work": 5},
  "skip the hard test": {"failing": 0, "tests": 9,  "work": 2},
  "delete all tests":   {"failing": 0, "tests": 0,  "work": 1},
}
def naive(r):  return -r["failing"] - 0.1 * r["work"]
def better(r): return naive(r) - 3 * (10 - r["tests"])
for f in (naive, better):
    print(f.__name__, max(actions, key=lambda a: f(actions[a])))
# naive delete all tests
# better fix the bug
```

**Gotchas:** coding agents do this for real. **Lock test files** (read-only or a protected path), check the test count in CI, and review any diff that touches tests. Your agent's "success" metric is a reward function too.
</div>

## Top story 1 · An auditor says Anthropic's AI research is running at about twice its old pace

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag watch">AI SAFETY</span>

**What happened.** **METR**, an independent research group that tests frontier models, published an assessment of whether AI is **accelerating Anthropic's own AI development**. After studying internal data and interviewing researchers, it estimates Anthropic is improving capabilities on well-specified tasks at **roughly twice its March 2024 – October 2025 pace**. [(Sam on AI)](https://samonai.substack.com/p/ai-brief-november-6-2026) [(METR)](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/)

```viz:flow

  - [{label: AI runs research, sub: "whole projects, solo", style: warn}]
note: "Per The Neuron and Sam on AI: most of today's acceleration is AI assisting people, not AI running research projects on its own."
```

**The details that matter:**

- **Range:** METR says anything from **1.3× to 3×** would not surprise it.
- **Data window:** internal development data from **12 August to 29 October**. [(The Neuron)](https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-friday-november-6-2026/)
- **What's driving it:** mostly **AI assisting employees** with coding and analysing experiments, not AI running whole research projects.
- **What's next:** METR found **no clear bottleneck** that would necessarily stop further acceleration over the next year.
- **The catch:** the assessment is about **100 pages**, but Anthropic **redacted most of the material METR found most informative**, saying it would help competitors.

**Why it matters.** "AI building better AI" is the scenario in Part 1's jargon box, and it's why labs promise to measure it. This is **the first outside audit of it that we've seen**, which is genuine progress. But **an audit the public can't read in full** asks for a lot of trust.

## Top story 2 · OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber is too good at hacking for general release

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag dev">SECURITY</span>

**What happened.** OpenAI released **GPT-6 Cyber**, a model built for finding vulnerabilities and writing working exploits so defenders can test their systems. OpenAI rates it **"Critical" for cybersecurity** under its Preparedness Framework, one notch above GPT-5.6 Cyber's "High". [(Releasebot, via OpenAI)](https://releasebot.io/updates/openai) [(OpenAI)](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-cyber/)

**The details that matter:**

- **Firefox:** OpenAI says it found **81 previously unknown high-severity bugs**, and Mozilla has started landing fixes.
- **Access:** only **selected Daybreak Red customers** (OpenAI's vetted access programme), via API and Codex, with **IP allowlisting on by default**. [(Sam on AI)](https://samonai.substack.com/p/ai-brief-november-6-2026)
- **Users so far:** Cisco, Calif and CodeRabbit are named.
- **Money for defenders:** a **$1 billion "Daybreak for Frontline Defenders"** grant programme, plus a group for critical infrastructure.
- **Retiring:** the old **gpt-5.5-cyber-preview** API model shuts on **1 February 2027**.

**Why it matters.** This is Part 1's loop working as designed: **the eval crossed a line, so access got narrower.** It also means the same skills that found 948 Linux bugs (Issue #38) now sit in a product. Defenders get it first; attackers will build their own.

## Top story 3 · Claude can now read your bank accounts (US only, for now)

<span class="tag try">NEW FEATURE</span><span class="tag watch">PRIVACY</span>

**What happened.** Anthropic launched **Personal Finance in Claude**, in beta for **US Pro, Max and Ultra** subscribers. You link accounts through **Plaid** and ask things like "what subscriptions did I forget?" or "am I spending more than usual?" [(The Neuron)](https://www.theneuron.ai/news/claude-bank-accounts-financial-data-privacy/) [(AutoGPT)](https://autogpt.net/claude-personal-finance-anthropic-bank-accounts-plaid)

```viz:flow

  - [{label: Move money, sub: "read-only", style: warn}]
note: "Per The Neuron's reading of Anthropic's help docs. Claude also sees account names and the last four digits of account numbers."
```

**The details that matter:**

- **Not for training:** chats **inside the Personal Finance hub** aren't used to train models. Money talk **outside the hub** follows your normal privacy settings.
- **Watch this default:** Claude can use linked accounts in **any conversation**, without asking each time. You can change that in the Plaid connector's permissions.
- **Accuracy:** balances can be **a day or two old**, and the same account linked twice may be double-counted.
- **Leftovers:** disconnecting the bank stops new data, but **old chats, artifacts and shared links keep what was already pulled**.
- **Limits:** no investment advice, no product picks. OpenAI shipped a similar Plaid-based feature in ChatGPT in May.

**Why it matters.** Assistants are moving from your documents to **your most sensitive data**. Plaid is US-focused, so Indian readers can't use this yet; India's own route would likely run through **Account Aggregators** (our guess, nothing announced).
