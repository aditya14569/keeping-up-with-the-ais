# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 📚 RAG: letting a model read your documents

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 3 · BUILDING WITH AI</span>

**A model only knows what it was trained on. RAG lets it look things up first, then answer.**

**RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)** has two halves. **Retrieval:** search your documents for the few passages that match the question. **Generation:** paste those passages into the prompt and ask the model to **answer only from them**. The last two issues built the parts: embeddings (Issue #17) turn text into numbers, and a vector database (Issue #18) finds the closest matches.

**Analogy:** an **open-book exam**. The student (the model) is smart but can't remember your company's refund policy. RAG is **flipping to the right page before writing the answer**, and citing the page number so the examiner can check.

```viz:flow

```

**The part people get wrong is the chunking.** Cut text too small and you split sentences in half; too big and **one chunk mixes several topics**, so it matches nothing well. Hugging Face's guide says to aim for **one idea per chunk**, measured in tokens, with about **10% overlap** between neighbours. [(Hugging Face)](https://huggingface.co/learn/cookbook/en/advanced_rag)

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Chunk:** a short slice of a document, the unit you embed and retrieve.
- **Top-k:** how many chunks you fetch per question (k = 4 is a common start).
- **BM25:** old-school keyword search. Good at exact terms like error codes, which **embeddings can miss**.
- **Reranker:** a second model that **re-sorts the retrieved chunks** by how well they really answer the question.
</div>

| If answers are... | Check first |
|---|---|
| **Confidently wrong** | Did the right chunk come back at all? Print the retrieved text |
| **Vague or generic** | Chunks too big, or top-k too high and drowning the answer |
| **Missing exact terms** (codes, names) | Add keyword search (BM25) next to embeddings |
| **Mixing up sources** | Add metadata filters: product, language, date |

**Why now?** It's the payoff of the last two issues. And today's top story is **web-scale RAG**: DeepSeek now sells the search step on its own, so agents can **retrieve from the live web** instead of your files.

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** upload a long PDF (a policy, a manual) to any chatbot. Ask a question, then ask **"quote the exact sentence and page you used."** If it can't, treat the answer as a guess.
- **Some code:** reuse yesterday's Chroma collection. Run `c.query(query_texts=[q], n_results=4)`, join the returned `documents` into a block, and send `"Answer only from these notes. If they don't cover it, say so."` plus the block and the question to any LLM API. **That's a working RAG bot.**
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: a RAG call with a filter**

```python
hits = col.query(query_texts=[q], n_results=4,
                 where={"lang": "hi"})  # metadata filter
notes = "\n\n".join(hits["documents"][0])
prompt = (f"<notes>\n{notes}\n</notes>\n"
          f"Answer from the notes only, cite them. Q: {q}")
```

**Gotchas:** Anthropic found that adding a **50–100 token summary of the source document to each chunk**, plus BM25 and a reranker, cut failed retrievals by **67%** in its tests. [(Anthropic)](https://anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval) **Retrieved text is untrusted input:** a document can carry instructions, and LangChain warns no prompt trick fully stops that injection. [(LangChain)](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langchain/rag.md) And **measure retrieval before tuning the prompt**; most bad answers start with bad chunks. Filter syntax: [(Chroma)](https://docs.trychroma.com/docs/querying-collections/metadata-filtering.md)
</div>

## Top story 2 · Anthropic: agents now work six hours for every human hour

<span class="tag watch">JOBS</span><span class="tag hot">DATA</span>

**What happened.** Anthropic published a study of **its own workforce**, built from internal hiring records, agent logs, surveys and 17 interviews. The headline: **agent working hours now outnumber human hours 6 to 1**, after first passing them in **March 2026**. [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic)

```viz:bars

  - {label: "Tech staff: latest", value: 14, display: "14%", highlight: true}
note: "Anthropic's report (Oct 16). Human hours are counted as headcount x 40 hours a week; agent hours exclude waits over 30 minutes."
```

**The details that matter:**

- **Output:** commits per engineer are **5x what they were six months earlier**. Comparing 192 engineers who adopted coding agents with 74 who didn't, Anthropic found **no rise in code later deleted or reverted**.
- **Who's coding:** non-technical staff went from **2 coding-agent sessions a month to 90**.
- **The new bottleneck is people:** interactive Claude sessions sit **idle about 80% of the time, waiting for a human**. Median meeting time rose 20%.
- **Hiring changed:** interviewers now score **autonomy and judgment** more and domain knowledge less.

**Why it matters.** It's rare data from inside an AI-first company. Anthropic flags the limits: early adopters were already more productive, and **output counts volume, not value**. Still, **fewer junior hires** is the line every fresher in India will notice, and Anthropic admits its own fixes **don't solve that**.

## Top story 3 · OpenCode breach puts stored AI keys at risk

<span class="tag dev">SECURITY</span><span class="tag watch">ACT NOW</span>

**What happened.** OpenCode, an open-source coding agent that lets you **plug in your own OpenAI, Anthropic or Google keys**, told some users their **saved provider keys may have been exposed**. An outsider got into a **legacy system the company was retiring**. OpenCode says **1.4% of its Console users** were hit, all of them people who had linked outside providers. [(Notebookcheck)](https://www.notebookcheck.net/OpenCode-hacked-users-urged-to-revoke-their-API-keys.1426957.0.html)

**The details that matter:**

- **The motive, per OpenCode:** a **"large-scale distillation attack"**: harvesting a model's answers to train a copycat model, **billed to your account**.
- **Real money:** users reported **$400 of Anthropic charges** and about **€200 of Google usage** they didn't make, some **before OpenCode's email arrived**. These are unconfirmed user reports.
- **OpenCode says** users without an email weren't affected; it calls the incident ongoing. [(Notebookcheck)](https://www.notebookcheck.net/OpenCode-hacked-users-urged-to-revoke-their-API-keys.1426957.0.html)

**Why it matters.** Every tool holding your key is **another place it can leak from**.
