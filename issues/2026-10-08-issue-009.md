# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🎯 Prompting fundamentals: role, context, examples, format

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 2 · GETTING GREAT RESULTS</span>

**A prompt is a briefing, not a search query. The model only knows what you tell it, so most bad answers start with a thin brief.**

When you type "write a bio for me", the model has to guess **who you are, who will read it, how long it should be and what tone you want**. It guesses the average. Good prompting just means **removing the guesswork**, and almost every good prompt has the same four parts.

**Analogy:** prompting is like **briefing a tailor**. "Make me a kurta" gets you *a* kurta. Give the occasion (**role and purpose**), your measurements (**context**), a photo of one you like (**examples**) and say "knee-length, by Friday" (**format**), and you get *your* kurta.

### The four parts, quickly

- **Role:** one sentence like "You're a senior Python reviewer" **focuses tone and expertise**. Put it in the system prompt.
- **Context:** the facts the model can't know, **and why each rule exists**. Anthropic's example: instead of "never use ellipses", explain the text will be **read aloud by a speech engine**. The model then handles similar cases itself.
- **Examples:** showing beats telling. Anthropic suggests **3–5 varied examples**; OpenAI suggests starting with none (zero-shot) and **adding examples only if needed**.
- **Format:** be precise. OpenAI's tip: **"3 to 5 sentences" beats "fairly short"**, and say what to do rather than only what not to do.

**The golden rule** from Anthropic's guide: **show your prompt to a colleague** who knows nothing about the task. If they'd be confused, the model will be too.

**Why now?** Models are changing how much hand-holding they need. OpenAI's GPT-6 builder guide says **overly specific guidance can now hinder results**: state the outcome, the reader and what "done" means, then let the model work. [(OpenAI)](https://openai.com/index/practical-guide-building-gpt-6/)

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **System prompt:** standing instructions (role, house rules) sent before the chat.
- **Zero-shot / few-shot:** asking with no examples, or with a few worked examples included.
- **Delimiters:** markers like `"""`, `###` or XML tags (`<context>…</context>`) that separate instructions from the material.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** pick one real task (a cold email, a WhatsApp reply to a client). Ask ChatGPT or Claude with a **one-line prompt**. Then open a new chat and add **role, context, one example and a format**. Compare the two answers side by side.
- **Some code:** ask the model to **improve your prompt** first: "Here's my prompt. What would you need to know to do this well? Rewrite it." Then run the rewrite.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: role in `system`, material in tags**

```python
import anthropic
ticket = open("ticket.txt").read()
client = anthropic.Anthropic()
msg = client.messages.create(
    model="claude-haiku-5-5", max_tokens=600,
    system="You are a support lead at a UPI payments startup. Be brief and kind.",
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content":
        "<rules>Refunds take 5-7 working days.</rules>\n"
        "<example>Q: Where is my refund? A: ...</example>\n"
        "<task>Reply in under 60 words.</task>\n"
        f"<ticket>{ticket}</ticket>"}])
print(next(b.text for b in msg.content if b.type == "text"))
```

- **Order for caching:** stable instructions and reference material **first**, task-specific bits last, so the repeated part can be cached. [(OpenAI)](https://openai.com/index/practical-guide-building-gpt-6/)

**Gotcha:** on Haiku 5.5, **thinking is on by default**, so `content[0]` may be a thinking block, not text. That's why the code above picks the first text block. [(Claude Docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview)
</div>

## Top story 1 · Claude Haiku 5.5: small, fast and very cheap

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag dev">DEVS · API</span><span class="tag try">TRY TODAY</span>

**What happened.** Anthropic released **Claude Haiku 5.5** (`claude-haiku-5-5`), the last of its 5.5 family. For prompts up to 100K tokens it costs **$0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output**, against $1 and $5 for Haiku 4.5. Anthropic says it's about **75% cheaper to run on average**. [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-haiku-5-5) [(9to5Mac)](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/07/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-haiku-5-5-model-details-here/)

```viz:bars
note: "Anthropic's own numbers, not independently verified."
```

**The details that matter:**

- **Long prompts cost more:** above 100K tokens the price rises to **$0.50 in / $2.50 out**. The context window is **1 million tokens**, with up to 128K output. [(Claude Docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview)
- **First Haiku with an effort dial,** so you can trade cost for intelligence. Anthropic says Sonnet and Opus are **still better for complex agentic coding**: on Terminal-Bench 4.0, Haiku 5.5 scores 39.2% against Sonnet 5.5's 70.6%.
- **Cheaper Sonnet too:** Sonnet 5.5 cache reads dropped from **$0.20 to $0.10** per million tokens, which Anthropic says cuts most agentic tasks by about 20%.
- **Free API credits for subscribers:** Max 5x gets **$100 a month**, Max 20x $200, and Team plans up to $500, pooled. [(Unite.AI)](https://www.unite.ai/anthropic-releases-claude-haiku-5-5-cutting-small-model-api-prices/)
- **Watch the fine print:** a new tokenizer means **the same text counts as more tokens**, so real savings are a bit smaller than list prices suggest.

**Why it matters.** Small models do the boring, high-volume work: **summarising, sorting, sub-agents, browser tasks**. A tenfold price cut makes **AI on every request** affordable.

## Top story 2 · GPT-6 for everyone: answers become apps

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag try">EVERYONE</span>

**What happened.** OpenAI began rolling out **GPT-6 with "Intelligent UI"** in ChatGPT. Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise got it on Wednesday; **Free and Go users get it today**. Paid plans run **GPT-6 Sol**; Free and Go run the lighter **GPT-6 Luna**. [(OpenAI)](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-for-everyone/)

**The details that matter:**

- **Answers can contain charts, tappable buttons, forms and small tools.** OpenAI's examples: a road-trip map, a bill splitter, a savings calculator, and **explainers where you change the inputs** (the Monty Hall problem, GDP).
- **The model chooses** when interactivity helps and when plain text is better. OpenAI built a **library of streamable components** and a compiler that draws the interface while the answer is still being written.
- **Faster, by OpenAI's internal tests:** on questions needing web search, GPT-6 Instant **starts answering 44% sooner** than GPT-5.6 Instant.
- **Not changing:** the models behind Work and Codex. OpenAI's post **mentions no API changes and no price changes**.

**Why it matters.** ChatGPT has **1.2 billion weekly users**, says OpenAI, and the cheapest plans get the upgrade a day after paid ones. **Go launched first in India**, so many readers here get GPT-6 today. For builders, the bar for "a good answer" is moving **from paragraphs to small interactive tools**.

## Top story 3 · Local-AI PCs priced, agents get a sandbox

<span class="tag dev">DEVS · HARDWARE</span><span class="tag watch">LOCAL AI</span>

**What happened.** At an event in San Francisco, Microsoft priced its first PCs built on **Nvidia's RTX Spark chip**, made to **run AI models on the device**. It also announced a revamped Windows 11 with **Execution Containers**, a way to sandbox AI agents, coming to all Windows 11 users. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/microsoft-releases-new-nvidia-chip-ai-pcs-with-revamped-windows-11/)

**The details that matter:**

- **The Dev Box is for builders:** it comes with VS Code, GitHub Copilot CLI, WSL and PowerShell 7. When it was first shown in June, Microsoft quoted **up to 128 GB of unified memory**, enough for big local models. [(Engadget)](https://engadget.com/2185865/microsoft-surface-rtx-spark-dev-box)
- **Execution Containers** let a developer or admin declare **which files, network and apps an agent may touch**. Windows then enforces it. A stricter mode cuts the agent off from your desktop, clipboard and keyboard. [(Petri)](https://petri.com/microsoft-execution-containers-boundaries-ai-agents/)

**Why it matters.** Local AI hardware is going mainstream (see Issue #8), and **so are agents running on your own PC**. A system-level sandbox is the safety net: an agent can only damage what it was allowed to reach.
