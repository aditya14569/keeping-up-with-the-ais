# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🔏 Watermarks: the invisible signature in AI text

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 7 · NEWER TECHNIQUES</span>

**You can't see it, you can't delete a character to remove it, and it's already in text that Claude and Gemini write.**

**The plain-English version.** When a model writes, it picks one word (token) at a time from many likely options. Often, **two or more options are about equally good**. A watermark uses those moments: a **secret key** quietly decides which option wins. One reply looks normal. But across hundreds of words, the choices form a **statistical pattern** that only someone holding the key can check.

**Analogy:** a cricket commentator with a secret habit. Whenever "brilliant" and "superb" both fit, he picks whichever **today's secret list** says. Listeners never notice. But if you hold the list and count his choices over a whole match, the pattern is unmistakable.

**The classic recipe (2023).** University of Maryland researchers proposed the **green list**. Before each word, a hash of the previous token plus a key splits the vocabulary: a share (say **25%**) is "green". The model gets a small **boost** to green words. Normal writing lands on green about 25% of the time. Marked text lands there **far more often**. [(arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.10226v4)

**The detector is just counting.** It works out a **z-score**: how far the green count sits above chance. The paper uses **z above 4** as "marked", which gives a false-alarm rate of about **3 in 100,000**. It says detection can work from **as few as 25 tokens**. And it never needs the model, only the key.

**What's in use now.** Google DeepMind's **SynthID Text** runs in Gemini. Its Nature paper reports a live test on **nearly 20 million Gemini replies**, with no significant difference in thumbs-up ratings. [(AI Weekly)](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/deepminds-synthid-text-watermark-ran-live-across-gemini) In August, Anthropic said **Claude's text carries a watermark based on SynthID**, with no hidden characters and no extra tokens. [(Android Authority)](https://androidauthority.com/how-claude-watermarks-text-3699100) Its **detection API** is in **private preview**, by request, for groups such as regulators, researchers and educators. [(Digital Applied)](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/claude-text-watermark-mythos-opus-sonnet)

**Why now?** The EU AI Act asks providers to mark AI-generated content. Anthropic signed the EU's **code of practice on AI-content transparency** in July and switched the mark on worldwide. [(The Decoder)](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-announces-watermark-detection-api-that-will-let-third-parties-detect-claudes-ai-texts/) OpenAI's EU text watermark (textGrain) was in Issue #7.

**The weak spots.** Google's docs say SynthID survives **cropping and a few word changes**, but **thorough rewriting or translation** can sharply cut confidence, and **factual answers** carry less signal (there's often only one right word). It is **not built to stop a motivated adversary**. [(Google)](https://ai.google.dev/responsible/docs/safeguards/synthid) Anthropic adds **short text and code** to that list.

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Watermark:** a hidden statistical pattern in word choices, checkable with a key.
- **Green list:** the share of words that get a boost at each step.
- **z-score:** how many "standard steps" a count sits above what chance predicts.
- **Paraphrase attack:** rewriting text (often with another AI) to wash the mark out.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** ask a chatbot for a one-line fact ("capital of Karnataka") and a 300-word story. Which one leaves **more room for word choice**? That room is where a watermark lives, and why facts and short replies are hard to mark.
- **Some code:** with `transformers` 4.46+, pass `watermarking_config=SynthIDTextWatermarkingConfig(keys=[...], ngram_len=5)` to `model.generate(..., do_sample=True)` on any small open model. [(Hugging Face)](https://huggingface.co/blog/synthid-text)
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: watermark and wash out, in 20 lines**

```python
import random, math
random.seed(0); V, G = 1000, 0.25
green = lambda prev, tok: hash((7, prev, tok)) % 4 == 0  # secret rule
def write(n, mark):
    s = [0]
    for _ in range(n):
        opts = random.sample(range(V), 8)  # 8 equally good words
        g = [w for w in opts if green(s[-1], w)]
        s.append(random.choice(g if mark and g else opts))
    return s
def z(s):
    T = len(s)-1; hits = sum(green(a, b) for a, b in zip(s, s[1:]))
    return (hits - G*T) / math.sqrt(T*G*(1-G))
# 25 tokens:  plain -2.0   marked 7.7
# 200 tokens: plain  0.3   marked 21.1
# marked 200, 25% words swapped: 13.1 | 50% swapped: 5.4
```

A toy (we ran it; it always picks green when it can, so its mark is far stronger than a real one). Still, the lesson holds: **longer text = stronger evidence**, and every edited word chips the score down.

**Gotchas:** SynthID's detector must be **trained per key**, and Hugging Face suggests **10,000+ examples**. **Keep the key secret**, or others can fake your mark. Anthropic's docs reportedly say Sonnet 4.6 may skip the mark if you change **temperature, top_p or top_k**. [(Digital Applied)](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/claude-text-watermark-mythos-opus-sonnet)

## Top story 3 · Hugging Face hands its chat routing to OpenRouter

<span class="tag dev">DEV</span><span class="tag try">TRY IT</span><span class="tag watch">OPEN MODELS</span>

**What happened.** Chat models on Hugging Face's **Inference Providers** now run **through OpenRouter**, which picks a provider for each request by live **price, speed and reliability**, and retries if one fails. That's **160 models from 38 labs**, and your existing Hugging Face token still works. [(OpenRouter)](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/hugging-face-inference-providers/)

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Three API styles:** OpenAI **Chat Completions**, OpenAI **Responses** and Anthropic **Messages**. Add **`:fastest`**, **`:cheapest`** or a provider name to the model ID.
- **The breaking change:** your own **provider API keys stop working for language models**. They still work for image and video. [(TestingCatalog)](https://www.testingcatalog.com/hugging-face-moves-inference-providers-routing-to-openrouter/)
- **Same price:** you pay the provider's price on your Hugging Face bill, **no markup**. PRO users keep **$2 a month** in credits.
- **Speed claims:** OpenRouter claims **46% fewer capacity errors**; TestingCatalog cites **16.9% faster first tokens**. Treat these as company numbers.

**Why it matters.** The two biggest front doors to open models now share **one router**. For you, that means fewer "provider down" errors, and **one less key to manage**.
