# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🎙️ Voice agents: why AI conversations pause, and how new models stop it

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 7 · NEWER TECHNIQUES</span>

**A good phone call has almost no gap between speakers. Most AI voice apps leave a second or two. That gap is the whole engineering problem.**

**The plain-English version.** A **voice agent** is an AI you talk to out loud: a support line, a booking bot, ChatGPT's voice mode. The classic way to build one is a **chain of three models**: speech-to-text writes down what you said, a text LLM decides the reply, and text-to-speech reads it out. Each step **adds delay**, and the app also has to guess **when you've finished talking**.

**Analogy:** a cricket commentator working through an interpreter. You speak Hindi, the interpreter writes it down in English, the expert answers in English, and a second interpreter reads it back in Hindi. It works, but **every hand-off costs a beat**, and tone of voice gets lost on paper.

```viz:flow

  - [{label: "STT → LLM → TTS", sub: "cascaded: 3 models"}, {label: Speech-to-speech, sub: "one model, audio in/out", style: accent}, {label: Full-duplex, sub: "listens while talking", style: soft}]
  - [{label: Speaker, sub: audio stream}]
note: "Our diagram of the three approaches OpenAI's voice-agents guide describes."
```

**Speech-to-speech** models take audio in and give audio out, with no text step in the middle. That saves hand-offs and keeps **tone, emotion and laughter**, which a transcript throws away. OpenAI's Realtime API and Google's Gemini Live API both work this way. [(OpenAI)](https://developers.openai.com/docs/guides/voice-agents)

**Full-duplex** goes one step further: the model **listens and speaks at the same time**, like people do. It can hear you say "haan, haan" without stopping, or stop mid-sentence when you cut in. The research paper that made this famous, Kyutai's **Moshi**, models your speech and its own as **two parallel streams**, with a theoretical latency of **160 ms (200 ms in practice)**. Cascaded systems, it notes, often take **several seconds**. [(arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00037)

**The hardest part is turn-taking.** Most apps use **VAD** (voice activity detection): if you go quiet for, say, 700 ms, it assumes you're done. Pause to think ("my PNR is... umm") and the bot **jumps in**. Wait longer and every reply feels slow. Smarter **turn models** listen to intonation and words to judge whether a sentence is actually finished. [(Pipecat)](https://docs.pipecat.ai/server/utilities/smart-turn/smart-turn-overview)

**Why now?** At Ignite on Tuesday, Microsoft announced **MAI Realtime 1**, its first speech-to-speech model, in limited preview from **$0.01 per audio minute** plus reasoning tokens. Microsoft claims it beats OpenAI's GPT Realtime 2.1; nobody has checked that independently yet. [(Scalevise)](https://scalevise.com/resources/microsoft-mai-realtime-1-pricing-preview-gpt-realtime-claims/)

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **STT / TTS:** speech-to-text and text-to-speech.
- **VAD:** voice activity detection; spots speech vs silence.
- **Barge-in:** the user interrupting while the bot is talking.
- **Full-duplex:** both sides can talk at once, like a phone call.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** open voice mode in ChatGPT or Gemini Live. Start a sentence, **pause for two seconds mid-thought**, then finish it. Does it interrupt you? Then **cut in while it's talking**. How fast does it stop?
- **Some code:** run the toy below and change `silence` from 700 to 300. Faster, yes, but in a real app it would start cutting people off.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: where the second goes, in 9 lines**

```python
# Toy latency budget for one voice turn (made-up times, ms)
pipes = {
  "cascaded":         {"silence": 700, "stt": 300, "llm": 450, "tts": 250, "net": 150},
  "speech-to-speech": {"silence": 700, "model": 500, "net": 150},
  "s2s + smart turn": {"turn_model": 250, "model": 500, "net": 150},
}
for name, stages in pipes.items():
    print(f"{name:18} {sum(stages.values()):>5} ms")
# cascaded 1850 ms | speech-to-speech 1350 ms | s2s + smart turn 900 ms
```

The lesson: the **silence timeout is often the biggest single item**. A faster model won't fix a bot that waits 700 ms to be sure you've stopped.

**Gotchas (Gemini Live API):** input audio is **16-bit PCM at 16 kHz**, output is 24 kHz. When VAD hears an interruption, the server **cancels the reply** and flags it `interrupted`; your client must **stop playback and clear queued audio** itself. If your mic stream pauses for over a second, send `audioStreamEnd`. The API is still **in preview**. [(Google AI)](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/live-guide?authuser=0) **Pipecat Smart Turn** is designed for **English**; test it on Hinglish before you rely on it.
</div>

**The options, side by side:**

| | Cascaded pipeline | Speech-to-speech | Full-duplex |
|---|---|---|---|
| How it works | STT, then LLM, then TTS | One model, audio in and out | Listens and talks at once |
| Examples | Any STT + LLM + TTS combo | OpenAI Realtime API, Gemini Live API, MAI Realtime 1 | Moshi (research), OpenAI's GPT-Live |
| Turn-taking | Your VAD or turn model | Server VAD, interruptions built in | No fixed turns at all |
| Best for | Control, logging, Indian-language STT | Natural, fast chat | Back-and-forth that feels human |
| Watch out for | Lag adds up across hand-offs | Harder to debug; preview APIs | Newest, fewest options |

<span class="tag dev">DEV</span><span class="tag try">TRY IT</span><span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span>

It costs **$3 in / $15 out** per million tokens and reads up to **1 million tokens**. <p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Independent scores:** Artificial Analysis gives Neon **61** on its Intelligence Index at its top "xhigh" setting, vs **59** for Opus 5.5 and **57** for GPT-6.2 Sol. The default "high" setting scores 60.
- **Four thinking levels:** low, medium, high (default) and xhigh. **Cost per task swings a lot** with the level; at low, the index score drops to 44.
- **Cheaper options:** batch costs **$1.50 / $7.50**; cached input is **$0.15**. There's **no free tier**, and Neon wasn't in Vertex AI's catalogue yet on Thursday.
- **A real-world check:** developer Flavio Copes found Neon **as accurate as Opus** on small coding tests but **slower and pricier per task** at default effort. It did better on a 100K-token search, partly because Gemini's tokenizer counted **~26% fewer tokens**. [(Flavio Copes)](https://flaviocopes.com/gemini-4-neon/)

**Why it matters.** Frontier-level coding at $3/$15 puts **price pressure on Opus 5.5** ($4/$20). Google's own claim of "#3" on Artificial Analysis didn't match the live ranking Beri saw, so trust **scores, not ranks**.
