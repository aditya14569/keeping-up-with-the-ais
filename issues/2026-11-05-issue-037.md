# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🧠 GPUs, TPUs and inference chips: why Cerebras is fast

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 5 · UNDER THE HOOD</span>

**When a model writes a reply, the chip spends most of its time fetching weights from memory, not doing maths. So the fastest AI chips are the ones that move data fastest.**

**The plain-English version.** To produce **each new token**, a model has to touch **every weight it uses**. For a 70B model at 8 bits, that's about **70 GB read from memory for every single word** of the reply. The maths per weight is tiny: one multiply and one add.

**A modern GPU can do far more maths than its memory can feed it.** An Nvidia H100 does about **989 trillion BF16 operations a second**, but its memory (called **HBM**) delivers "only" **3.35 TB/s**. [(InferenceX)](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/chips/h100) So while one person chats, the maths units mostly sit waiting. Engineers call this being **memory-bandwidth-bound**.

**Analogy:** **a Mumbai dabbawala crew with one handcart.** The crew can sort and deliver tiffins at lightning speed, but every tiffin first has to come in on the cart from the kitchen. **Hire faster sorters and nothing changes. Build a wider road to the kitchen and everything does.** Chip designers are mostly building wider roads.

**This gives you a handy rule.** The most tokens per second one user can get is roughly **memory bandwidth ÷ model size in bytes**. Plug in a few chips and the ranking jumps out.

```

**So why is Cerebras fast?** Instead of cutting a silicon wafer into hundreds of chips, it uses **the whole wafer as one chip**: 46,225 mm², about **57 times an H100**. It keeps the model in **44 GB of on-chip SRAM** (the super-fast memory right next to the cores) with **21 PB/s** of bandwidth. [(TweakTown)](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/96843/cerebras-wse-3-wafer-scale-ai-chip-57x-bigger-than-largest-gpu-with-4-trillion-transistors/index.html) **That's thousands of times the H100's road.** The catch: 44 GB is small, so big models are spread across several wafers.

note: "A systolic array passes numbers through a grid of multipliers like a bucket brigade, so each weight is reused many times once fetched."
```

**Why now?** Chips are all over this week's news. Qualcomm's **150-watt Cloud AI 100 Ultra** card (128 GB of LPDDR4x at 548 GB/s) hit Hacker News. AMD's filing prices its purchase of inference-chip startup **Taalas at about $2.9B**. <div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **HBM:** high-bandwidth memory, stacked right beside a GPU. **Big and fast**, but still the bottleneck.
- **SRAM:** memory **on the chip itself**. Much faster, much smaller and pricier per GB.
- **Memory-bandwidth-bound:** speed is limited by **how fast weights arrive**, not how fast you multiply.
- **Batching:** serving many users at once, so **one weight fetch serves everyone**. That's how providers make GPUs pay.
</div>

**🛠️ For devs: estimate speed before you buy**

```python
def tok_per_sec_ceiling(params_billion, bits, bandwidth_gb_s):
    model_gb = params_billion * bits / 8      # bytes read per token
    return bandwidth_gb_s / model_gb

for chip, bw in {"Qualcomm AI 100 Ultra": 548,
                 "H100": 3350, "8x H100": 8 * 3350}.items():
    print(f"{chip:22s} {tok_per_sec_ceiling(70, 8, bw):5.0f} tok/s")
# Qualcomm AI 100 Ultra      8 tok/s
# H100                      48 tok/s
# 8x H100                  383 tok/s
```

**Gotchas:** for **mixture-of-experts** models (Issue #35), use the **active** parameters, not the total. Software matters too: per AI Weekly, an **RTX Spark laptop** ran GPT-OSS-120B at **65.4 tok/s on Windows but 13.9 on Linux**, because of locked power settings.

## Top story 1 · Update: Kimi K3.1 Pro's weights land, and Tencent opens Hy4

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag dev">OPEN WEIGHTS</span>

**What happened.** Moonshot kept its promise: **Kimi K3.1 Pro's weights are on Hugging Face**, late on 4 November (we went to press without them yesterday). It's a **2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model** that activates **104B per token**, with a **1M-token context** and built-in vision. [(AI Weekly)](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/moonshot-ships-kimi-k31-pro-28t-moe-with-1m-context) [(Hugging Face)](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro)

**The details that matter:**

- **Built for 4-bit:** weights were trained to run in **MXFP4** with 8-bit activations, the "quantization-aware training" from yesterday's Learn.
- **Experts:** **896 experts, 16 picked per token** plus 2 shared, across 93 layers.
- **Claimed scores:** **65.7 on Terminal-Bench 4.0** and 94.2 on GPQA-Diamond. These are Moonshot's own numbers; **no independent tests yet**.
- **Licence:** a **custom "Kimi K3.1 Pro License"**, not Apache or MIT. Read it before you build a product on it.
- **Same day:** Tencent released **Hy4, a 770B open-weights MoE** with a 1M context and computer-use skills, on Tencent Cloud and OpenRouter, with a **50% launch discount until 31 December**. [(AI Weekly)](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today)

**Why it matters.** Today's Learn maths in action: 104B active at about 4 bits is **roughly 52 GB read per token**, and all 2.8T weights (about **1.4 TB at 4 bits**, our maths) must sit in memory. That's a rack of GPUs. **For almost everyone this is an API model**, but the open weights mean many hosts will compete on its price.

## Top story 2 · Claude found two critical Xen hypervisor bugs

<span class="tag hot">SECURITY</span><span class="tag dev">PATCH NOW</span>

**What happened.** The Xen Project's advisory **XSA-523** credits **Anthropic's Claude** with finding **CVE-2026-102703 and CVE-2026-102704**. Both let a **malicious guest VM read and write other VMs' memory**, including dom0, the control VM that runs the host. [(AI Weekly)](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/xen-credits-claude-for-critical-grant-copy-vm-escape-flaws) [(The Hacker News)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/xen-hypervisor-flaws-let-one-malicious.html)

**The details that matter:**

- **Where:** Xen's **grant-copy** code, which copies memory between VMs. A shortcut that cached already-checked page numbers **re-checked them wrongly** in some cases.
- **Who's hit:** **every Xen release from 4.6 on**. There are no patches for 4.6 to 4.16, which are out of support.
- **Fixes:** XCP-ng 8.3 and Qubes OS 4.3 have updates; both need a **host or dom0 restart**.
- **Checked by humans:** Ada Logics validated the findings, and **CodeAnt AI reported the same bugs independently**. The patch carries an "Assisted-by: Claude." line.
- **Unknown:** Anthropic hasn't said **how much a human steered** the search.

**Why it matters.** A hypervisor is the wall between strangers' VMs on a shared server. **AI models are now finding serious bugs in mature, heavily reviewed software.** That helps defenders, but the same skill helps attackers.

## Top story 3 · "You don't need long context," says a new paper

<span class="tag try">TRY IT</span><span class="tag dev">FOR DEVS</span>

**What happened.** A new paper tests whether agents really need giant context windows. It finds that **compacting the history** (replacing old turns with a short summary) keeps accuracy within **−3.7 to +5.6 points** of keeping everything, while cutting costs **6.5 to 54.6 times** on long tasks. [(AI Weekly)](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/arxiv-you-dont-need-long-context-paper-finds-36-7x-cost-savings-from-compaction) [(Hugging Face Papers)](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.05306)

```viz:versus
left: {emoji: 📜, title: Keep everything, points: ["Every tool call and reply stays in the prompt", "Cost grows every turn", "256K context costs **3.6–7x** more than 16K"], example: "turn 40: 180K tokens re-read"}
right: {emoji: 🗜️, title: Compact, points: ["Swap old turns for a **short written state**", "Prompt stays small", "Accuracy about the same, per the paper"], example: "turn 40: 12K tokens + summary"}
note: "Token counts in the examples are illustrative. The 3.6–7x and accuracy figures are from the paper, as summarised by AI Weekly."
```

**The details that matter:**

- **Two tricks:** compact once a conversation ends (**EoCC**), or after **every single action** (**SAC**).
- **Tested widely:** SWE-bench, Terminal-Bench 2.1 and LongBench v2, on models including Claude Opus 4.5, GLM 5.2, Kimi K2.6 and Gemma 4.
- **A new idea:** "**semantic state space models**", which carry a compact plain-English state forward instead of the whole transcript.

**Why it matters.** Long context is **expensive and slow** (see Part 1: more tokens, more memory traffic). If a summary does the job, **your agent bill could shrink a lot** without a model change.
