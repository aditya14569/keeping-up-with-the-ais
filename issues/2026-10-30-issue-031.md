# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🧑‍🏫 Distillation: how big models teach small ones

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 4 · SHIPPING FOR REAL</span>

**The cheapest way to get a small model that's good at your job is to let a big one teach it.**

**Distillation** means training a small "student" model to copy a big "teacher" model. The teacher answers thousands of prompts. The student learns from **those answers, not just the original data**, so it picks up the teacher's habits in a much smaller package.

A **small language model (SLM)** is roughly anything you can run on a laptop, a phone or one cheap GPU: think **under 10 billion parameters**. Distillation is one of the main ways labs build them.

**Analogy:** **a senior chef writing down every dish for a new cook.** The new cook doesn't need 30 years in the kitchen. They study the chef's actual plates, and the reasons behind them. They'll never match the chef on everything, but on **the ten dishes your dhaba serves**, they can get very close, and cost far less per shift.

```viz:flow
title: "How distillation works"
headers: [1 · Prompts, 2 · Teacher, 3 · Student, 4 · Ship]
columns:
  - [{label: Your task inputs, sub: "thousands", style: soft}]
  - [{label: Big model, sub: "answers + reasons", style: accent}]
  - [{label: Small model, sub: "learns to copy", style: accent}]
  - [{label: Fast + cheap, sub: "laptop, phone, API", style: soft}]
note: "Simplified. Some methods copy only the teacher's final text; others copy its full probability scores for every token."
```

**Two flavours.** The simple one: have the teacher **write answers**, then fine-tune the student on them (Issue #30). The stronger one copies the teacher's **probabilities**: not just "the next word is *blue*", but "80% blue, 15% grey". That extra signal teaches the student how confident to be. NVIDIA's guide describes this as copying the teacher's **logits** (raw scores). [(NVIDIA)](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-to-prune-and-distill-llama-3-1-8b-to-an-nvidia-llama-3-1-minitron-4b-model)

```viz:versus
left: {emoji: 📝, title: Copy the answers, points: ["Teacher **writes outputs**, student fine-tunes on them", "Works with **any API model** as teacher", "Easiest to start"]}
right: {emoji: 🎯, title: Copy the probabilities, points: ["Student matches the teacher's **scores per token**", "Needs the teacher's **weights**, usually open models", "Usually **stronger** for the same data"]}
note: "Both are common. Copying probabilities usually needs teacher and student to share a vocabulary (MaxText docs)."
```

**How small can you go?** In Google's "distilling step-by-step" research, the teacher wrote out **its reasoning as well as its answers**. A **770M-parameter** student then beat a few-shot **540B PaLM** on a benchmark, using only **80% of the training examples**: over **700x smaller**. [(Google Research)](https://research.google/blog/distilling-step-by-step-outperforming-larger-language-models-with-less-training-data-and-smaller-model-sizes/)

**The catch:** a student is great at **what it was taught** and weaker everywhere else. That's why it's worth it for one narrow, high-volume job, not a general chatbot.

**Why now?** Mistral says **smaller distilled versions** of yesterday's 1T model are coming "in the coming months", **including one for laptops**. [(TNW)](https://thenextweb.com/news/mistral-large-4-open-weights-release) Distillation is how frontier models trickle down to hardware you own.

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Teacher / student:** the **big model** that knows the task and the **small one** learning from it.
- **Logits:** the model's **raw scores for every possible next token**, before they become probabilities.
- **KL divergence:** a number for **how different two probability lists are**. Distillation tries to shrink it.
- **Pruning:** **cutting layers or neurons** from a model, often followed by distillation to recover quality.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** pick a task you repeat. Ask a big model to answer **20 examples, explaining its reasoning**. Paste 5 of those into a small, cheap model's prompt as examples and test the rest. If it does well, you've just found **a job a student could learn**.
- **Some code:** run the TRL **GKD trainer** example below on a free Colab GPU. It uses a 1.5B teacher and a 0.5B student, both small enough to fit.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: distillation in TRL**

```python
from trl import GKDConfig, GKDTrainer
from transformers import AutoModelForCausalLM, AutoTokenizer
tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen2-0.5B-Instruct")
student = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen2-0.5B-Instruct")
teacher = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen2-1.5B-Instruct")
args = GKDConfig(output_dir="gkd-model", per_device_train_batch_size=1)
GKDTrainer(model=student, teacher_model=teacher, args=args,
           processing_class=tok, train_dataset=ds).train()  # ds: chat "messages"
```

**Gotchas:** pick teacher and student from **the same family** so their tokenizers match. TRL's `lmbda` setting controls how much the student learns from **its own attempts**; the GKD authors found more of that works better. **Check the teacher's licence**: some API terms forbid using outputs to train competing models. And evaluate the student on **your eval set** (Issue #26), not the teacher's benchmarks.
</div>

## Top story 1 · Mistral opens up its trillion-parameter model

<span class="tag hot">OPEN WEIGHTS</span><span class="tag dev">CODING</span><span class="tag watch">LICENCE</span>

**What happened.** Mistral published the weights for **Mistral Large 4**, nicknamed "Le Chonk", on Hugging Face. It's a **mixture-of-experts** model of about **1.05 trillion parameters**, but only about **49B are active per token** (52B counting embeddings), which keeps it cheaper to run. It reads text and images and writes text and code. [(OrcaRouter)](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/mistral-large-4-open-weights-licence)

```viz:bars

  - {label: Kimi K3, value: 21, display: "21%"}
note: "Company-reported, via TNW. No third party has re-run the released checkpoint yet (OrcaRouter)."
```

**The details that matter:**

- **Better than the preview:** Terminal-Bench went from **28% to 41%** after three extra weeks of RL training, Mistral says. It claims **47% fewer tokens per task** than GLM-5.3 on coding agents. [(TNW)](https://thenextweb.com/news/mistral-large-4-open-weights-release)
- **Big download:** the main repo is **1,060.6 GB** and ungated. A compressed **NVFP4** version is **610 GB** and, per Mistral, runs on **four Nvidia B200 or B300 GPUs**.
- **The licence catch:** you can use, modify, fine-tune and sell products built on it. But if you run a **"Model as a Service" business**, renting the model out to others, you need a **separate commercial licence**. Internal use and features built into your own product are exempt.
- **API price:** a launch promo of about **$0.70 input / $2.10 output** per million tokens; OrcaRouter lists the full price at roughly double that. Context is listed at up to **1M tokens**.

**Why it matters.** Mistral's science VP pitched it simply: once downloaded, the weights **can't be cut off** by anyone. For most readers, the real prize is what comes next: **distilled versions small enough to run locally**.

## Top story 2 · The browser inside your coding agent isn't a real browser

<span class="tag hot">SECURITY</span><span class="tag dev">FOR DEVS</span><span class="tag watch">UNPATCHED</span>

**What happened.** Gal Weizman of Forever Security tested the **built-in browsers** in Cursor, GitHub Copilot, OpenAI Codex, VS Code and Claude Desktop, the little preview panes where your agent opens web pages and local HTML files. The research, called **EMBRACE**, found every one broke **at least one rule a normal browser enforces**. [(Help Net Security)](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/29/research-ai-assistant-browser-vulnerabilities/)

| Tool | What the attack did | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Cursor** | A page hid a command; one approval ran it, and **the agent switched off its own sandbox** to retry | Patched Oct 1 |
| **GitHub Copilot** | A page could **fake the address bar**, e.g. a phishing page shown as github.com/login | Fixed in 1.1.29 |
| **Claude Desktop** | A local HTML file could **read files outside the project** | Second report accepted by Anthropic |
| **VS Code** | Same file-reading trick | **Unpatched**; Microsoft declined |
| **OpenAI Codex** | Reads any file; no folder limit at all | **Unpatched**; no reply since July |

**The details that matter:**

- **What leaked in tests:** **SSH keys, .env files, npm tokens** and the hosts file. [(Forever Security)](https://forever.security/blog/embrace-hacking-5-ai-assistants-embedded-browsers)
- **The trigger is ordinary:** a project's README says "set up", the assistant offers a link to an HTML file, **you click once**.
- **Vendors disagree:** Microsoft and, at first, Anthropic said "Workspace Trust" (the "do you trust this folder?" prompt) is the protection. Weizman says that prompt gives **"zero protection."**

**Why it matters.** Vibe coders clone strangers' repos all the time, and **the agent itself draws the link you click**. Your agent's browser pane has your files, but none of the decades of protections Chrome has.

- Keep secrets out of plain files where you can: a **password manager or secrets CLI** beats `.env` on disk. Rotate keys if you've opened unknown repos.

## Top story 3 · OpenAI rewrites its rulebook around five values

<span class="tag watch">POLICY</span><span class="tag learn">HOW MODELS BEHAVE</span>

**What happened.** OpenAI published a new version of its **Model Spec**, the public document describing how its models should behave. It's now built around **five values**: faithfulness to human intent, minimising harm, honesty and transparency, human agency, and care. **Faithfulness comes first**: grasp what people actually want, not just the literal words. [(TNW)](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-model-spec-five-values-update)

```viz:versus
left: {emoji: 📘, title: August Spec, points: ["Calls the model **\"the assistant\"**", "Lots of **detailed style rules**", "81 headings"]}
right: {emoji: 📗, title: October Spec, points: ["Calls the model **\"GPT\"**", "Most style rules **removed**", "70 headings, **five values** on top"]}
note: "Heading counts are FourWeekMBA's own comparison of the two versions, not OpenAI figures."
```

**The details that matter:**

- **Defaults, not commandments:** most rules are now **defaults that users and developers can change**, with firm limits only where needed to protect people.
- **New sections** on using **memory and past chats**, respecting people's own choices, and following social norms.
- **OpenAI's own admission:** its models **"do not yet fully reflect"** the Spec, and it is testing new training methods. It's asking the public for feedback. [(FourWeekMBA)](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-openai-rebuilds-model-spec-around-5-values-cuts-11-headings/)

**Why it matters.** It's a shift from rules to **judgment**, since rulebooks can't cover every action an agent takes. After yesterday's agent-steering study, note that **"human agency"** made the five.
