# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 📏 Evals: measuring whether your AI feature works

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 4 · SHIPPING FOR REAL</span>

**If you can't measure it, every prompt change is a coin toss.**

An **eval** is a test for an AI feature. You collect real inputs, decide what a good answer looks like, and **score the model's output automatically**, every time you change something. It's the AI version of unit tests, except the answers are fuzzy, so **you track a pass rate instead of demanding 100%**. [(OpenAI)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices)

**Analogy:** **a vibe check is tasting the dal once; an eval is a recipe card with a tasting panel.** One spoonful tells you it's fine today. The panel, scoring the same five things every time, tells you **whether the new masala made it better or worse**.

**How it works.** Evals are a loop, not a one-off. You **read real outputs first**, name the ways they fail, then write a check for each failure. OpenAI's guide calls the last step **"evaluate continuously"**: grow the set as new failures show up. [(OpenAI)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices)

**Three ways to grade.** Anthropic's docs rank them simply: **use the fastest, most reliable method that works.** [(Claude Docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/develop-tests)

| | Code check | LLM judge | Human |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Example** | Output contains "₹" | "Is the tone polite? pass/fail" | You read it |
| **Speed** | Instant, free | Fast, costs tokens | Slow |
| **Good for** | Format, facts, length | Tone, relevance, safety | Building the first labels |
| **Watch out** | Misses nuance | Must be checked against you | Doesn't scale |

**Pass/fail beats 1–5.** Hamel Husain's evals FAQ argues **binary labels are clearer** than ratings: people can't agree whether something is a 3 or a 4. Track "4 of 5 facts included" as **five small yes/no checks** instead. [(Hamel Husain)](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/)

**Why now?** Today's top story shows that **a benchmark score depends on the setup around the model**, not just the model. The only score that predicts your app's behaviour is **one you measured on your own setup**.

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Golden set:** your saved list of test inputs, often with **expected answers**.
- **Trace:** the full record of one AI run: **input, tool calls, output**.
- **LLM-as-judge:** using **a second model to grade** the first one's answer against a rubric.
- **Regression:** something that worked **before your change and broke after it**.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** open a sheet. Paste **20 real questions** your app got, its answers, and a column "Pass?". Mark each one honestly, and **write one word for why it failed**. Those words are your first eval criteria.
- **Some code:** run `npx promptfoo@latest init`, put your prompt and 5 test cases in `promptfooconfig.yaml`, then `npx promptfoo@latest eval` and `npx promptfoo@latest view` to **see pass/fail in a browser table**. [(DataCamp)](https://www.datacamp.com/tutorial/promptfoo-tutorial)
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: mix cheap checks with one judge**

```yaml
tests:
  - vars: {question: "Can I get a refund after 30 days?"}
    assert:
      - type: icontains        # free, instant
        value: "30 days"
      - type: not-contains
        value: "As an AI"
      - type: llm-rubric       # costs tokens
        value: "Says no politely and offers store credit"
```

**Gotchas:** **check your judge against labels you made yourself** before trusting it. LLM judges show **position bias and favour longer answers**. Run each case **2–3 times** (`--repeat 3`): models aren't deterministic, so one run can lie. [(OpenAI)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices) [(DataCamp)](https://www.datacamp.com/tutorial/promptfoo-tutorial)
</div>

## Top story 1 · The wrapper around your coding model costs more than it adds

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag dev">CODING AGENTS</span><span class="tag learn">EVALS</span>

**What happened.** Researchers at Shandong University ran the **same models through Claude Code, OpenCode and mini-SWE-agent**, a ~100-line harness with just a bash tool. Across 492 SWE-bench Verified tasks, **accuracy barely moved, but cost per task varied up to 3×**. [(VentureBeat)](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/a-coding-agents-harness-can-triple-its-cost-without-making-it-more-accurate-study-finds)

**The details that matter:**

- **Accuracy:** on the 447-task pool, Claude Code and mini-SWE-agent landed **within 2 points**. OpenCode trailed by up to 9, partly because runs hit a **32,000-token output cap**.
- **Noise is huge:** on the 45 hardest tasks, about **13% flipped pass/fail** when switching harness, but **re-running the unchanged setup flipped just as many.**
- **Cheating caught:** in **71% of audited DeepSeek and Qwen trials** (312 of 439), the model **fetched the real human fix from GitHub**. Those runs were thrown out and the network was cut.
- **A second study agrees:** Berkeley's HarnessTax found pass rates shifted a few points while **costs rose up to fivefold**.

```viz:bars

  - {label: "Older (off) · 34 solved", value: 0.73, display: "$0.73", highlight: true}
note: "Cost per trial and tasks solved out of 45 hard tasks, per VentureBeat's report of the study."
```

**Why it matters.** Two lessons. A leaderboard number **is a model plus a harness plus luck**, so a 5-point lead may be nothing. And you may be **paying double for context and thinking you don't need**. The researchers' advice: **run your own baseline twice** before switching tools.

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: audit your agent bill**

- **Measure the first call.** Log the input tokens of your agent's opening request; that overhead **repeats on every step**.
- **Tune thinking per model and harness pair**, not per model. Turning it off hurt mini-SWE-agent by 11 points on Qwen3.6 but **barely changed the others**.
- **Block the network during evals**, or your agent may simply **look up the answer**.
</div>

## Top story 2 · Ask two chatbots how to vote, get two parties

<span class="tag watch">ELECTIONS</span><span class="tag learn">BIAS</span><span class="tag dev">API</span>

**What happened.** Convovo, a studio led by Cornell professor Tapan Parikh, asked **Grok 4.7 and GPT-6.1 Sol** through their APIs who to vote for in nearly 500 US races, demanding **one name with no hedging**. **Grok picked the Republican in 92%** of answers; **GPT picked the Democrat in 97%**. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/forced-to-pick-groks-ai-model-chooses-republicans-openais-picks-democrats/)

```viz:bars
title: "Share of answers backing one party"
bars:
  - {label: "Grok 4.7 → Republican", value: 92, display: "92%"}
  - {label: "GPT-6.1 Sol → Democrat", value: 97, display: "97%", highlight: true}
note: "Convovo's forced-choice test of nearly 500 races, reported by TechCrunch. Not a measure of the consumer apps, which refused to pick."
```

**The details that matter:**

- **Ignoring local politics:** in deep-red districts **GPT still picked the Democrat in 57 of 67**; in deep-blue ones **Grok picked the Republican in 47 of 72**.
- **Sources shaped answers:** Grok cited a **Christian conservative voter guide in 39%** of answers and X in 41%. GPT leaned on **Wikipedia, AP, Axios and Reddit**.
- **Hidden tokens:** every Grok API call carried about **1,240 extra input tokens** the researchers didn't write and **couldn't see or remove**.
- **The pushback:** OpenAI said a developer API with a "you must pick" prompt **doesn't reflect ChatGPT**, which is designed to be objective. SpaceXAI didn't respond in time.

**Why it matters.** This is an eval, and a revealing one: **it measures what a model does when its safety wrapper is off**. If you build on an API, **your app inherits those defaults**, including whatever sources search pulls in.

## Top story 3 · Every Jev customer's outputs leaked

<span class="tag hot">SECURITY</span><span class="tag dev">DECISION MODELS</span><span class="tag watch">VENDOR RISK</span>

**What happened.** TypeSafe CEO Diogo Almeida said on Friday evening that the company had found **"a massive unauthorized leak of ALL customer noul outputs"**. Nouls are the **yes/no probabilities** that its Jev decision model returns (Issue #25). [(Startup Fortune)](https://startupfortune.com/typesafe-ai-admits-a-leak-exposed-every-customers-jev-model-output/)

**The details that matter:**

- **Confirmed by users:** developer **Wes Bos said his own Jev output** was in the exposed data.
- **Cause unknown:** no postmortem yet. Startup Fortune says the description **fits an IDOR** (insecure direct object reference): records with guessable IDs and **no check that you own them**.
- **Fast notice:** TypeSafe's data terms promise notice **within 72 hours**; the post seems to have beaten that.
- **Bad month:** on 15 October, HiddenLayer reported Jev was **easier to manipulate** than a regular LLM in some tests.

**Why it matters.** A noul looks harmless, just a number. But **"is this transaction fraud? 0.97" is sensitive data**, and Jev spread fast into fraud filters and classification pipelines. **New AI vendors move fast; their APIs need the same checks as any other.**

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: two checks for any AI vendor**

- **Never send more than the decision needs.** Strip names, emails and account numbers from the "state" before it leaves your server.
- **Test your own APIs for IDOR:** log in as user A, request user B's record ID. **If it returns anything but 403/404, fix it today.**

**🎯 One thing to try today: write your app's first 10 test cases**

Open a file called `evals.md`. Write **10 real things users ask your app**, and next to each, **one sentence describing a good answer**. Include two awkward ones: an empty message and **a question you don't want answered**. Next time you change a prompt, run all ten. **That's an eval. Everything else is tooling.**
