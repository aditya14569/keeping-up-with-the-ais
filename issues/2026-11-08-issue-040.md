# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## ⚖️ AI regulation around the world (incl. India)

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 6 · THE BIGGER PICTURE</span>

**There is no single "AI law." Each region has its own rules, and your app follows the rules wherever its users are.**

**The plain-English version.** Governments rarely regulate "AI" in general. They regulate **uses** (hiring, credit, deepfakes), **outputs** (labels on synthetic content) and, increasingly, **the physical stuff** (chips and data centres).

**Analogy:** **road rules.** The EU wrote one thick driving code for every road type. India mostly uses its existing traffic laws, plus a few sharp rules for the worst drivers. The US lets each state set speed limits, while Washington argues over whether they should.

| Region | Style | What actually binds today |
|---|---|---|
| **EU** | One big law, sorted by risk | Bans since **Feb 2025**; rules for big general-purpose models since **Aug 2025** |
| **India** | Existing laws + targeted rules | **IT Rules amendment** in force since **20 Feb 2026**: label synthetic content, quick takedowns |
| **US** | Patchwork of state laws | State laws, with a federal push to **challenge them** (DOJ task force, Issue #39) |
| **China** | Many narrow, specific rules | Labelling and registration rules, plus **reviews of foreign deals** |

**The EU's ladder.** The **EU AI Act** sorts systems by risk. The higher the rung, the heavier the paperwork. In May, EU lawmakers provisionally agreed to **delay the high-risk rules**: to **2 Dec 2027** for stand-alone systems and **2 Aug 2028** for AI built into regulated products. The transparency rules (telling people they're talking to AI, marking synthetic media) kept their **2 Aug 2026** start. [(Gibson Dunn)](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)

**India's version.** India chose **not to write a standalone AI law** in its November 2025 AI Governance Guidelines. It relied on the IT Act, the data protection (DPDP) Act and rules for each sector, with voluntary commitments first. [(ThePrint)](https://theprint.in/india/governance/india-steps-up-ai-regulation-push-with-consultation-paper-likely-next-month-deepfakes-safety-in-focus/3065386/) The first binding AI-specific duties came through the **IT Rules amendment**, effective **20 Feb 2026**. [(CMS)](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/ai-regulation-scanner/india)

- **Label it:** tools that create synthetic content must label it **visibly or audibly** and add a **permanent metadata identifier**.
- **Take it down fast:** platforms get **three hours** to remove flagged synthetic content after a court or government order (two hours for non-consensual intimate images), or risk losing their **"safe harbour"** protection.
- **Big platforms** (5 million+ users) must ask users to **declare synthetic uploads** and check those declarations.

**Why now?** India is about to go further. On 8 Oct, IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said a **regulation-focused consultation paper** would come "in a month's time", covering safety and deepfakes, and said industry would carry most of the responsibility. [(ThePrint)](https://theprint.in/india/governance/india-steps-up-ai-regulation-push-with-consultation-paper-likely-next-month-deepfakes-safety-in-focus/3065386/) We haven't seen it published yet. Meanwhile, today's story 3 shows the newest kind of rule: **limits on data centres**.

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Safe harbour:** legal protection for platforms from liability for **what their users post**, as long as they follow the rules.
- **High-risk AI (EU):** systems used for decisions like **hiring, credit or exams**, which need testing, logs and human oversight.
- **SGI:** India's term for **"synthetically generated information"**, i.e. AI-made or AI-altered content.
- **Preemption:** a national law **overriding state laws** on the same topic.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** list every place your app **shows AI output to a user**. For each one, ask: does the user know it's AI? Is AI-made media labelled? That covers most of the EU and India transparency duties.
- **Some code:** run the snippet below to add a visible label and a metadata tag to a generated image.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: label AI images in 12 lines**

```python
from PIL import Image, ImageDraw
from PIL.PngImagePlugin import PngInfo
img = Image.new("RGB", (800, 450), "steelblue")  # your AI image
d = ImageDraw.Draw(img)
d.rectangle([0, 405, 800, 450], fill="black")     # label band
d.text((12, 418), "AI-generated", fill="white")
meta = PngInfo()
meta.add_text("ai_generated", "true")
meta.add_text("generator", "my-app/1.0")
img.save("out.png", pnginfo=meta)
print(Image.open("out.png").text)
# {'ai_generated': 'true', 'generator': 'my-app/1.0'}
```

**Gotchas:** a PNG text tag is **easy to strip**, so it's a starting point, not compliance. CMS says Indian practice is converging on **C2PA** content credentials, which are signed and harder to remove. Keep a server-side log of what you generated, too.
</div>

## Top story 1 · DeepMind's AI agents find two new cracks in old RSA encryption

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag dev">SECURITY</span>

**What happened.** Google DeepMind said on 6 Nov that AI agents, steered by cryptographers **Nadia Heninger and Emmanuel Thomé**, found **two new attacks on legacy 1024-bit RSA**, the encryption that has protected logins, signatures and certificates for decades. It took **weeks**, DeepMind says. [(Crypto Briefing)](https://cryptobriefing.com/google-deepmind-ai-rsa-1024-bit-weaknesses/) [(AI Weekly)](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today)

```viz:flow

note: "Per AI Weekly's summary of DeepMind's 6 Nov announcement. The DeepMind post itself was not available to us."
```

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Attack 1:** forging signatures on 1024-bit RSA with **short digests** becomes roughly **1,600× more efficient**.
- **Attack 2:** once you've factored one 1024-bit key, each **additional key in a batch** costs about **30× less** to break.
- **Not affected:** **RSA-2048** and larger, which is what modern systems use.
- **Team:** led at DeepMind by **Dj Dvijotham and Abhradeep Thakurta**.
- **Context:** in September, Heninger and Thomé co-wrote a separate paper showing **1024-bit RSA signatures can be forged** through a hardware signing device, without stealing the key. That paper recommends **moving away from RSA entirely**. [(IACR ePrint)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2131)

**Why it matters.** 1024-bit RSA was already considered weak, but it **still lives on in old devices, firmware and internal systems**. The bigger signal: after OpenAI's Linux bugs (Issue #38), **AI agents are now doing real cryptography research too**, with experts steering.

**👀 Should you care?** Only if you run old infrastructure. **Developers:** find any 1024-bit keys and replace them.

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: find weak keys**

Check a key's size with `openssl rsa -in key.pem -noout -text | head -1` and a site's certificate with `openssl s_client -connect host:443 </dev/null | openssl x509 -noout -text | grep "Public-Key"`. Anything under **2048 bits** should go. For new systems, prefer **elliptic-curve keys** (Ed25519, P-256).
</div>

## Top story 2 · Anthropic explains how Claude remembers, and why it "dreams"

<span class="tag try">AGENTS</span><span class="tag dev">FOR BUILDERS</span>

**What happened.** Anthropic published a research post on **the parts of agent memory**: how an AI agent keeps useful knowledge from one session to the next. It describes **three layers**, ending with **"dreaming"**, where memory gets cleaned up while the agent is idle. [(AI Weekly)](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/anthropic-research-unveils-three-part-agent-memory-architecture-lifts-opus-55) [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **The headline number:** with memory on, **Opus 5.5 scored 0.833 on CL-Bench, up from 0.644**, Anthropic says.
- **Less is more:** in a Pokémon-playing test, **Claude Fable 5 earned four badges using 10 focused memory files**, while Sonnet 3.7 got **one badge from 413 files**.
- **Not brand new:** "dreaming" first appeared in May as a **research-preview feature of Claude Managed Agents**, which merges duplicate memories and drops outdated ones. Teams can approve or reject the result. [(YourStory)](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/anthropic-claude-dreaming-self-improving-agents)

**Why it matters.** Most agent frustration is **forgetting**: re-explaining your project every session. Anthropic's lesson is that **a few well-chosen notes beat dumping everything into memory**. That holds for your own `CLAUDE.md` or rules files too.

## Top story 3 · Towns and countries start saying no to data centres

<span class="tag watch">POLICY</span><span class="tag hot">GROWING FAST</span>

**What happened.** In 48 hours, the pushback against AI data centres went global. Several hundred activists **occupied Microsoft's half-built data centre in Amsterdam**, and Dutch police detained **150–200 people**. It kicked off a two-day "Stop Dirty Data Centres" campaign with about **85 actions across 15 European countries**. [(Al Jazeera)](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/6/protesters-break-into-microsoft-data-centre-site-in-amsterdam) [(AI Weekly)](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/activists-storm-microsofts-amsterdam-ai-data-center-as-stop-dirty-data-centres)

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Thailand:** suspended **49 half-built data centres** in September and froze approvals for **117 projects** while it writes rules on power, water and siting. [(The Guardian)](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/07/datacentres-southeast-asia)
- **Malaysia:** its prime minister blocked new applications **not tied to AI**.
- **Massachusetts:** Northampton's council voted **unanimously** for an immediate **one-year pause**, and rejected a small-facility exemption 7–1. [(Daily Hampshire Gazette)](https://gazettenet.com/2026/11/06/northampton-city-council-unanimously-approves-yearlong-moratorium-on-ai-data-centers/)
- **The money angle:** Goldman Sachs estimates about **45% of planned US capacity** sits in states whose new Democratic governors campaigned on building limits. [(FourWeekMBA)](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-deutsche-bank-data-center-limits-could-cap-an-ai-bubble/)
- **Chips too:** Microchip says AI buyers have taken so much factory capacity that its **lead times went from 4–8 weeks to 6–32 weeks**. [(24/7 Wall St.)](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/07/the-chip-shortage-is-back-and-microchip-says-ai-customers-are-crowding-everyone-out/)

**Why it matters.** This is Part 1's newest kind of regulation. **Local power, water and zoning rules** may limit AI more than any AI Act does. Fewer new data centres could mean **tighter capacity and higher prices** for the APIs you build on.
