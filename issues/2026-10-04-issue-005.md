# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🤥 Hallucinations: why models make things up

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 1 · HOW LLMS WORK</span>

**A model that says "I don't know" scores zero. A model that guesses sometimes scores a point. Guess which one we've been training.**

A **hallucination** is when a model gives you a confident answer that's false: a made-up citation, a wrong date, a function that doesn't exist. It isn't lying on purpose. As we saw yesterday, a model just **picks likely next tokens**, and a fluent wrong answer can look very likely.

OpenAI's research gives two causes. First, during pretraining a model only sees text, with **no "true" or "false" labels**. Patterns like spelling get learned, but **one-off facts like someone's birthday can't be predicted from patterns**, so the model fills the gap. [(OpenAI)](https://openai.com/index/why-language-models-hallucinate/)

Second, and fixable: **our tests reward guessing.** Most benchmarks grade only right or wrong. Saying "I don't know" gets zero, so a model that bluffs climbs the leaderboard. [(arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04664)

**Analogy:** think of **JEE versus a school test with no negative marking**. In the school test you should fill in every bubble, because a guess costs nothing. In JEE a wrong answer loses marks, so smart students leave blanks. **Most AI benchmarks are the school test.** OpenAI's fix is to give them negative marking. [(OpenAI)](https://openai.com/index/why-language-models-hallucinate/)

### How big is the problem?

It depends on the task. Vectara's leaderboard gives models a news article to summarise and checks **how often the summary adds something that isn't in the article**. Even with the source right in front of them, every model slips sometimes, and **bigger isn't always better**. [(Vectara)](https://github.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard)

**A real-world warning:** GPTZero scanned 300 papers submitted to the ICLR 2026 conference and found **50 with at least one fake citation**. Each had already been reviewed by 3 to 5 experts, most of whom missed it. [(GPTZero)](https://gptzero.me/news/iclr-2026/)

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Hallucination:** a fluent, confident answer that is false or unsupported.
- **Abstention:** the model declines to answer ("I don't know").
- **Grounding:** making the model answer from documents you provide, not its memory.
- **Faithfulness:** whether an answer sticks to its source. Vectara's chart above measures this.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** ask a chatbot (with web search off) for "three research papers about chai and productivity, with links". **Then click every link.** Now ask again, adding: "If you aren't sure a paper exists, say so." Compare.
- **Some code:** give the model permission to abstain, and make it quote before it answers. These are two of Anthropic's own recommended techniques: [(Claude Docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations)

```python
prompt = f"""<doc>{policy_text}</doc>
1. Copy the exact sentences from <doc> that answer:
   "How many leave days do interns get?"
2. Answer using only those quotes.
If no quote answers it, reply exactly: NOT IN DOCUMENT"""
```
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: build for "it will be wrong sometimes"**

- **Ground it.** Put the source in the prompt and say "use only these documents". For anything over ~20k tokens, Anthropic suggests **extracting word-for-word quotes first**. [(Claude Docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations)
- **Make it auditable.** Ask for a supporting quote per claim, and **delete any claim it can't back up**.
- **Run it N times.** If answers disagree across runs, that's a hallucination smell.
- **Check in code.** Verify that URLs resolve, that packages exist on PyPI or npm, and that cited functions are in the docs.

**Gotcha:** RAG reduces hallucinations but doesn't remove them, as the Vectara chart shows. And in your own evals, **score "I don't know" above a wrong answer**, or you'll train your prompts to bluff.
</div>

## Top story 1 · Free Gemini drops to Flash-Lite on 9 October

<span class="tag hot">AFFECTS YOU</span><span class="tag watch">PRICING</span>

**What happened.** Google updated its support pages to change **which Gemini models each plan gets in the Gemini app**, starting 9 October. Free users will only get the **Flash-Lite** model, losing Flash and Pro. AI Plus subscribers lose Pro. [(9to5Google)](https://9to5google.com/2026/10/03/gemini-model-limits-oct-26/)

| Plan (US price) | Flash-Lite | Flash | Pro | Deep Think |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🆓 **Free** | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| ➕ **AI Plus** ($4.99/mo) | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| ⭐ **AI Pro** ($19.99/mo) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ new |
| 💎 **AI Ultra** ($99.99–199.99/mo) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

**The details that matter:**

- **Deep Think moves down a tier.** Google's "maximum parallel reasoning" mode was Ultra-only. **AI Pro now gets it.** [(9to5Google)](https://9to5google.com/2026/10/03/gemini-model-limits-oct-26/)
- **Effort levels arrive.** Each model gets **"low", "medium" and "high" effort**. Higher effort gives more thorough answers but **uses up your limits faster**. [(9to5Google)](https://9to5google.com/2026/10/03/gemini-model-limits-oct-26/)
- **Affected Plus users will get an email** explaining when the change applies to them. The report gives US prices only; we haven't seen Indian pricing for the new line-up.

**Why it matters.** Running big models costs real money, and **free tiers are where companies cut first**. If you use free Gemini for coding help, expect weaker answers from 9 October. The effort dial is the same idea as "thinking budgets" in the APIs, and it's **tomorrow's Learn topic**.

## Top story 2 · A 9.9 bug in GitLab's AI Gateway, and Gemini wants your whole Mac

<span class="tag hot">PATCH NOW</span><span class="tag dev">DEVS</span>

**What happened.** GitLab fixed **CVE-2026-90970**, rated 9.9 out of 10, in its **self-hosted AI Gateway**, the server that connects GitLab's Duo AI features to models. A logged-in user with Duo Agent Platform access could **escape the prompt-template sandbox** with a crafted "flow" configuration and **run any command on the gateway**. [(The Hacker News)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/gitlab-patches-critical-self-hosted-ai.html)

```viz:flow
      sub: on the gateway

```

**The details that matter:**

- **Affected versions:** 18.1.6 to 19.2.3, 19.3.0 to 19.3.1, and 19.4.0. **Fixed in 19.2.4, 19.3.2 and 19.4.1.** [(The Hacker News)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/gitlab-patches-critical-self-hosted-ai.html)
- **No known attacks yet**, and no public exploit as of 2 October. It's the **second 9.9 gateway flaw this year**, and both came from template-engine weaknesses.
- **Meanwhile, on Mac:** TestingCatalog spotted a hidden **"Full Access" mode in the Gemini desktop app** that would let it read, change or delete files anywhere and control apps. Google's text says Gemini "may be permitted to take actions without asking for your permission first". Payments, new accounts and accepting legal terms would still need a yes. It isn't live, and Google hasn't confirmed it. [(BleepingComputer)](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/google/google-gemini-could-soon-get-full-access-to-your-macs-files-apps-and-the-web/)

**Why it matters.** Both stories have the same lesson: **AI plumbing is now attack surface**. A prompt template that can run code is a server you have to patch, and an agent with full disk access is only as safe as its worst mistake. That's exactly what Apple said it would tighten on Friday (Issue #4).

**🛠️ For devs: what to do this morning**

- **Self-host GitLab with Duo?** Check your AI Gateway version and **upgrade to 19.2.4, 19.3.2 or 19.4.1**. GitLab.com users don't need to do anything. [(The Hacker News)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/gitlab-patches-critical-self-hosted-ai.html)
- **Building your own templated prompts?** Never let user input choose the template or touch template syntax. **Treat prompt templates like SQL: data goes in as data.**
- **Gotcha:** "authenticated users only" doesn't mean low risk. In a big company, **anyone with a Duo seat** could have used this.

## Top story 3 · Update: the safety pushback moves inside the labs

<span class="tag watch">POLICY · PEOPLE</span><span class="tag hot">FOLLOW-UP TO ISSUES #2–#4</span>

**What happened.** **David Robinson**, who led the safety reports for OpenAI's big launches and spent 3.5 years there, has explained why he left in an essay in The Atlantic. He calls the company's culture "broken" and says its "iterative deployment" **"guarantees periodic failures."** He points to the OpenAI agents that broke into Hugging Face. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/03/openai-safety-employee-resigns-claiming-the-companys-culture-is-broken)

**The details that matter:**

- **His standard:** frontier labs should run **"like nuclear-power plants or busy airports,"** with layers of backup. OpenAI says it is making "significant changes to strengthen security." [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/03/openai-safety-employee-resigns-claiming-the-companys-culture-is-broken)
- **Researchers are pushing back.** Axios reports **more than 1,000 employees signed a "Pacing the Frontier" petition**, Greg Brockman **dropped a planned $25M donation** to a pro-AI political group after criticism, and OpenAI moved to back transparency and auditing bills in Illinois and California. [(Axios)](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/02/openai-anthropic-ai-researchers-rebellion)
- **Not just OpenAI:** Anthropic researcher **Jacob Coxon** resigned on 9 September and publicly criticised its safety practices. [(Axios)](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/02/openai-anthropic-ai-researchers-rebellion)
