# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🧬 Synthetic data: when models write their own textbooks

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 7 · NEWER TECHNIQUES</span>

**The internet is running out of good, fresh text. So labs now ask one model to write training material for another.**

**The plain-English version.** "Synthetic data" just means training examples that **a model generated** instead of a human. You prompt a strong model ("write a beginner's lesson on fractions", "invent 10 tricky support emails and reply to each"), filter what comes back, and train on the result. It's used **everywhere** now: for teaching small models, for instruction tuning, and for filling gaps where real data is rare or private.

**Analogy:** **a senior teacher writing a workbook for a new class.** The workbook can be cleaner and better ordered than a pile of random library books. But if the teacher has blind spots, **every student inherits them**. And if those students later write the next workbook, the blind spots get bigger.

```viz:flow

  - [{label: Student model, sub: "learns from it", style: soft}]
note: "Simplified from Hugging Face's Cosmopedia write-up."
```

**The proof it works.** Microsoft's **phi-1** (1.3B parameters) was trained on 6B tokens of filtered web code plus **1B tokens of textbooks and exercises written by GPT-3.5**. In just 4 days on 8 A100 GPUs, it scored **50.6% on HumanEval**, a coding benchmark, and 55.5% on MBPP. [(arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11644) Earlier, **Self-Instruct** had GPT-3 write its own instructions and gained **33 points** on a standard instruction benchmark, close to OpenAI's InstructGPT-001. [(arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560)

**The hard part is variety, not volume.** Hugging Face built **Cosmopedia** (30M+ files, 25B tokens) with Mixtral, using **over 10,000 GPU hours**. They mixed 4 audiences (kids to researchers) with 3 styles (textbook, blog, wikiHow). Just changing the topic **wasn't enough to stop duplicates**, and "Once upon a time" kept turning up. Most of their time went on **prompt engineering**. [(Hugging Face)](https://huggingface.co/blog/cosmopedia)

**The danger: model collapse.** A 2024 *Nature* paper showed that training each generation **only on the last generation's output** causes "irreversible defects": the **tails** (rare facts, unusual phrasing) vanish first. [(Oxford OATML)](https://oatml.cs.ox.ac.uk/publications/202407_Shumailov_ModelCollapse.html) We ran a tiny version of this in our sandbox:


**Why now?** Cheap models like Gemini 4 Hydrogen (adapted from the bigger Carbon) keep closing the gap with flagships, and teaching small models with big-model output is one standard way to get there. Meanwhile the web is filling with AI text. **Knowing what's real** is becoming a competitive edge.

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Teacher / student:** the model that writes the data, and the one trained on it.
- **Decontamination:** removing anything that overlaps benchmark questions.
- **Distillation:** a student learning to copy a teacher's answers.
- **Model collapse:** training on model output, round after round, loses the **rare edges**.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** ask a chatbot for **20 fake customer complaints** about a food-delivery app. Count how many feel the same. Then ask again with 4 personas (a student in Pune, a busy parent, a retiree, a restaurant owner) and **compare the variety.**
- **Some code:** run the snippet below and watch the spread shrink.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: the collapse demo, and the fix**

```python
import random, statistics as st
data = [random.gauss(0, 1) for _ in range(50)]
for gen in range(300):
    mu, sd = st.mean(data), st.pstdev(data)
    data = [random.gauss(mu, sd) for _ in range(50)]
print(sd)  # ours: 1.13 at start, 0.00 by gen 300
```

Swap the last line of the loop so 10 of the 50 points are always **real samples**, and our run held at **1.01**. Cosmopedia dropped any sample that matched a benchmark question too closely (a 10-word overlap check, then a text-similarity ratio above 0.5). [(Hugging Face)](https://huggingface.co/blog/cosmopedia)

**Gotchas:** **keep real data in the mix**. **Vary personas and formats**, not just topics. **Check the teacher's licence**: some providers' terms limit training competing models on their outputs. **Spot-check facts**: Hugging Face found Mixtral hallucinated in its maths and Khan Academy subsets.
</div>

## Top story 1 · ChatGPT Wallet: agents that can actually pay

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag watch">AGENTS</span>

**What happened.** OpenAI launched **ChatGPT Wallet**, which lets its "dots" agents finish a purchase for you, not just find the item. It's starting with **a limited number of US ChatGPT Pro subscribers** aged 18+ with a US-issued Visa, Mastercard or Amex card. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/chatgpts-new-wallet-lets-ai-agents-shop-and-check-out-for-you/)

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Your card number stays hidden.** The card network issues a **payment token**, a stand-in card number, security code and expiry, so neither the agent nor the shop sees your real card. [(9to5Mac)](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/13/openai-launches-chatgpt-wallet-enabling-dot-agents-to-make-purchases/)
- **A human check on every purchase.** You review the items, the total and the card, then approve with **Face ID or a fingerprint**. Each approval leaves a record.
- **Rewards and disputes still work**, OpenAI says, as with a normal card payment.
- **Coming later:** spending limits and rules by category or time period, so you can **pre-approve** some purchases. Not available at launch. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/chatgpts-new-wallet-lets-ai-agents-shop-and-check-out-for-you/)
- **Not every shop is open.** It won't work on sites that block agents. TechCrunch notes **Amazon** is the largest retailer to ban them.

**Why it matters.** Agents that buy aren't new: TechCrunch notes Meta's Muse and Instinct already do. OpenAI's angle is **safety by design**: the agent never sees your card, and you sign off every time. No India timeline yet.

## Top story 2 · Claude for Manufacturing: 17 skills for the factory office

<span class="tag dev">FOR DEVS</span><span class="tag watch">ENTERPRISE</span>

**What happened.** Anthropic launched **Claude for Manufacturing**, a plugin with **17 skills** for estimators, quality engineers, technicians and buyers. It drafts **quotes, inspection reports (NCRs, 8Ds), RFQs and production reports** from a plant's own records, and a person reviews each one. [(Manufacturing Dive)](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/anthropic-launches-claude-for-manufacturing/874303/) [(Claude)](https://claude.com/solutions/manufacturing)

```viz:versus

note: "From Anthropic's product page and Manufacturing Dive."
```

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Four connectors:** Autodesk Fusion, Infor, Microsoft 365 and Oracle NetSuite. Other systems can feed in through exported files. [(TokenPost)](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/technology/56243)
- **Infor goes further:** its own **Infor Industry AI for Claude** plugin can change records, within Infor permissions, for example re-sourcing a purchase order and rescheduling production **after approval**. [(Dataconomy)](https://dataconomy.com/2026/11/13/anthropic-launches-claude-manufacturing-plugin-with-17-ai/)
- **Who gets it:** paid plans, including Enterprise. Reports differ on whether **Team** plans qualify. Admins can require approval before any connector changes data.
- **Setup by chat:** say "set us up" and Claude asks which systems you run, tests the connectors and lists what's missing.
- **No price or customer names** were published. A technical webinar is set for **17 Nov**.

**Why it matters.** The pitch isn't robots. It's the **paperwork around the machines**. Dataconomy cites a 2025 survey where **79% of manufacturers** said they still enter data by hand.
