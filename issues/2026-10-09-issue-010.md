# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🧱 Structured outputs and JSON mode

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 2 · GETTING GREAT RESULTS</span>

**Chat answers are for people. Structured outputs are for code: the model fills in a form you designed, so your app can read the answer without guessing.**

Say you want a model to read customer emails and pull out **name, email and whether they asked for a demo**. Ask in plain words and you get a friendly paragraph, sometimes with the fields in a different order, sometimes with "Sure! Here's the info:" on top. **Your code breaks on the day the wording changes.**

The fix is to ask for **JSON** (a simple text format of keys and values that every language can read) and, better, to hand the model a **schema**: the exact list of fields, their types and which are required.

**Analogy:** asking a chat model for data is like asking someone to **write their details on a blank sheet of paper**. JSON mode gives them **lined paper**. Structured outputs give them the **IRCTC booking form**: fixed boxes, a dropdown for the class, a date field that only takes dates. Nothing can land in the wrong box.

### How it works under the hood

A model writes **one token at a time**. With structured outputs on, the provider turns your schema into rules and, at each step, **only lets the model pick tokens that keep the output valid**. This is called **constrained decoding**. It's why the result matches by construction, not by luck. [(Respan)](https://www.respan.ai/articles/openai-structured-outputs-vs-json-mode)

**Why now?** "Typed answers" are becoming a product of their own. OpenAI's new **Decisions API** (beta since 6 October) returns a probability or a pick from fixed options instead of text, and Liquid AI just released **open-weight decision models** that do the same on a laptop. [(Unite.AI)](https://www.unite.ai/openai-releases-decisions-api-in-public-beta-powered-by-gpt-6-luna/) [(Liquid AI)](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/d1-open)

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **JSON:** text like `{"name": "Riya", "age": 29}`; keys on the left, values on the right.
- **JSON Schema:** a description of what valid JSON must look like (fields, types, required ones).
- **Pydantic / Zod:** popular Python / TypeScript libraries for defining a schema as a class; SDKs convert them for you.
- **Constrained decoding:** the model is only allowed to choose tokens that fit the schema.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** paste three of your WhatsApp order messages into ChatGPT or Claude and ask: "Return only JSON: a list of objects with `item`, `quantity` (number) and `deliver_by` (date or null)." Paste the result into any online JSON viewer. **Notice where it guessed.**
- **Some code:** run the snippet below on 10 real emails. Then remove the schema, ask in plain words, and count how often the parsing breaks.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: Pydantic in, typed object out (Claude)**

```python
import anthropic
from pydantic import BaseModel

class Lead(BaseModel):
    name: str
    email: str
    plan_interest: str
    demo_requested: bool

client = anthropic.Anthropic()
resp = client.messages.parse(
    model="claude-haiku-5-5", max_tokens=1024,
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": f"Extract the lead: {email_text}"}],
    output_format=Lead)
print(resp.parsed_output)  # Lead(name='…', demo_requested=True)
```

OpenAI's equivalent is `client.responses.parse(..., text_format=Lead)`, read from `output_parsed`. [(OpenAI)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs)

**Gotchas:** in raw schemas, list every field as required and set `additionalProperties: false`. Always check for a **refusal or `max_tokens` stop**: both can return output that doesn't match. On Claude, the raw API field is now `output_config.format`; the old `output_format` field is deprecated, and citations can't be combined with it. Compare enum values case-insensitively. [(Claude Docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)
</div>

## Top story 1 · Gemini becomes a coworker, not a chatbox

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag watch">AGENTS</span>

**What happened.** At its Gemini at Work event, Google Cloud announced the **Gemini agent**: one agent for work that answers questions, writes and runs code, and carries out tasks that **keep running in the cloud for hours or days** after you close your laptop. Businesses get it first; consumers later, with **no date given**. [(Google Cloud)](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/welcome-to-gemini-at-work-2026) [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/google-brings-agentic-ai-to-gemini-starting-with-businesses)

**The details that matter:**

- **"Coworker agents"** get their **own Workspace account**: an email address, calendar, Drive and a directory entry. You assign work by tagging or emailing them, and they **only see what's shared with them**.
- **Model choice:** it routes each job to a model on cost and quality. It already orchestrates **Anthropic's Claude** alongside Gemini; other private and open models are planned.
- **Runs everywhere:** web, phones, Windows, Mac, the command line, Workspace, Microsoft 365 and Slack, and connects to **any MCP server**.
- **Spend caps per project** pause an agent when its budget runs out. Google gave **no price and no launch date**; VentureBeat notes there are **no independent reliability tests** yet. [(VentureBeat)](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/google-cloud-unveils-persistent-gemini-agents-for-long-running-tasks-and-they-get-their-own-gmail-calendar-and-drive-storage)
- **Scale claims:** Sundar Pichai says Gemini has **over 1 billion monthly users** and nearly **90% of the Fortune 100** use Gemini Enterprise.

**Why it matters.** The race has moved from "best chatbot" to **"best digital employee"**. Google's pitch is that the agent has an identity, an audit trail and a budget, which is exactly what companies ask about before letting software act alone.

## Top story 2 · OpenAI puts a price on speed

<span class="tag dev">DEVS · API</span><span class="tag try">TRY TODAY</span>

**What happened.** OpenAI added an **Ultrafast mode for GPT-6.1 Sol** to the Responses API, **open to all API users**. You switch it on per request with `service_tier: "ultrafast"`; it shortens the gap between generated tokens. [(OpenAI)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog) [(OpenAI Devs on X)](https://x.com/OpenAIDevs/status/2108262830348886150)


**The details that matter:**

- **Speed claims are maximums:** OpenAI says **up to 6x faster** in the API and up to 8x in Codex, with no guaranteed response time. [(RuntimeWire)](https://runtimewire.com/article/openai-gpt-6-1-sol-ultrafast-api-pricing)
- **Our maths:** a call with 2,000 tokens in and 1,000 out costs about **$0.014 on standard, $0.084 on Ultrafast**.
- **Separate rate limits:** 1 million tokens a minute on the Build tier, 4 million on Launch, 40 million on Grow. Cached input is $0.60 per million.
- **In ChatGPT Work and Codex** it's narrower: Pro 500, eligible usage-based Enterprise and credit-based Edu plans, and **Enterprise admins must switch it on**.

**Why it matters.** Speed used to come from picking a smaller model. Now you can **keep the smart model and pay for it to hurry**, only on the calls where a person is waiting: a live demo, an agent clicking through an app, an outage at 2 a.m.

**🛠️ For devs: make speed a routing decision**

Don't turn Ultrafast on everywhere. Route by situation: **Ultrafast for user-facing turns**, standard for background jobs, and a small model (or a decision model) for classification. Log `service_tier` per call so your bill makes sense at month end.

## Top story 3 · Anthropic offers free bug hunts, and rewrites its rules

<span class="tag watch">SECURITY</span><span class="tag dev">OPEN SOURCE</span>

**What happened.** Anthropic launched the **Cyber Mission**, starting with two programmes. A **Critical Infrastructure Defense Program** gives Claude models, on-site engineers and threat research to firms protecting power grids, water and transport, with **11 founding partners** including CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Dragos and Hitachi. And an **OSS Scanner** scans open-source projects **for free**. [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-cyber-mission)

**The details that matter:**

- **Reports are machine-made and unreviewed,** so some may be wrong (for example, a mis-rated severity). Anthropic says it's for projects **with capacity to keep up with findings**; others keep getting human-verified disclosures.
- **New usage policy, effective 12 November:** a new section bans **deceptive campaigns** (fake reviews, astroturfing, bots posing as humans), Claude may not decide **who to investigate or arrest**, and when Claude controls hardware, **a qualified person must be ready to stop it**. It also bars "sustained and needless" cruelty towards Claude. [(MacRumors)](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/08/anthropic-user-guideline-update/) [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update)
- **Science money:** Anthropic committed **$150 million over three years** to the US Genesis Mission, bringing Claude to 15+ agencies including NASA and NIH. [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/genesis-mission-commitment)

**Why it matters.** AI is now **finding bugs faster than volunteers can fix them**. Offering scans only to projects that can keep up is an honest admission of that bottleneck.
