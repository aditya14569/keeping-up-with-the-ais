# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🔬 AI for science: biology, maths proofs and lab agents

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 7 · NEWER TECHNIQUES</span>

**Chatbots answer questions. Science AI tries to answer questions nobody has answered yet, and the hard part is proving it got them right.**

**The plain-English version.** AI is now used in three big ways in science. It **predicts** things that are slow to measure (like a protein's 3D shape). It **proposes** ideas for humans to test (like which drug might work). And it **proves** things in maths, where a computer can check every step. **Each needs a different way of checking the answer**, and that check decides how far you can trust it.

**Analogy:** a young researcher in a lab. A good one **reads everything, suggests experiments and writes up results**. But the professor still asks: "Did you run the control? Show me the data." Science AI is that researcher, and **verification is the professor**.

| | Predict | Propose | Prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Example | AlphaFold protein shapes | Google's Co-Scientist | Lean-checked proofs |
| Who checks? | Lab experiments, confidence scores | Human scientists in the lab | A proof checker, line by line |
| Speed of checking | Weeks to months | Months | Minutes to hours |

**Real example 1: lab agents.** Google DeepMind's **Co-Scientist** runs a team of agents: one generates hypotheses, one acts as a "virtual peer reviewer", and one runs an **Elo-style tournament** to rank ideas. In a Stanford liver-fibrosis project, one suggested drug **blocked 91%** of a scarring-linked response in lab tests, the company says. [(Google DeepMind)](https://deepmind.google/blog/co-scientist-a-multi-agent-ai-partner-to-accelerate-research/)

**Real example 2: agents plus proofs.** In a new paper, DeepMind teams of **Gemini 4 Carbon agents** designed cheaper quantum programs. They shared a wiki and held scheduled "conferences". An independent checker accepted a result only if its **Lean proof** passed. [(arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09822)

**Why now?** Story 1: Anthropic just published a proof that **no human has read**, checked only by Lean. Science AI has reached the point where **the checker is doing the trusting**.

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Lean:** a programming language for maths. If a proof compiles, every step follows logically.
- **Formal specification:** the exact statement you want proved, written in Lean.
- **Hypothesis:** an educated guess that an experiment can confirm or kill.
- **Protein structure:** the 3D shape a protein folds into, which decides what it does.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** play the first world of the **Natural Number Game** in your browser. You'll prove `0 + n = n` with Lean and feel how strict a proof checker is.
- **Some code:** look up any protein you've heard of (insulin, say) in the **AlphaFold Protein Structure Database** and check its colour-coded confidence score.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: a checker only checks what you asked**

```python
def spec(f):                      # meant to say "f sorts the list"
    out = f([3, 1, 2])
    return all(out[i] <= out[i+1] for i in range(len(out)-1))
cheat = lambda xs: []             # returns nothing at all
print(spec(sorted), spec(cheat))  # True True
# fix: also require sorted(out) == sorted(input) -> cheat fails
```

A toy we ran in the sandbox. The spec forgot to say "same items", so **an empty list counts as sorted**.

**Gotchas:** this is exactly the gap Anthropic flags. Lean checks the proof, but **someone has to check the statement means what the paper claims**. When agents write code against your tests, the same rule applies: **read the tests harder than the code**.
</div>

## Top story 1 · Claude wrote a proof no human has checked

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag learn">SCIENCE</span>

**What happened.** Anthropic added a folder to its public **formal-math** GitHub repo: a 70-page paper called **"VP ≠ VNP over every field"**, credited to **"Claude (Anthropic)"**, plus a Lean formalisation of about **73,000 lines in 324 files**. The README says an **internal research Claude model** found the result, and that **no human mathematician has checked the proof**. [(CASRAI)](https://casrai.org/news/anthropic-claude-authored-vp-vnp-proof-unverified-lean-2026)

**What's the problem?** In 1979 Leslie Valiant asked whether some polynomials are fundamentally harder to compute than others. It's the **algebra version of P vs NP**, not P vs NP itself. Anthropic's README says outright that it **"does not settle P vs NP"**. [(cellcog)](https://cellcog.ai/blog/claude-vp-vnp-proof/)

```viz:versus
left:  {emoji: ✅, title: "Checked by machine", points: ["Lean accepts every proof step", "A second, independent checker agrees", "Only standard maths axioms used"]}
right: {emoji: ❓, title: "Not checked yet", points: ["Does the Lean statement match the paper?", "No human expert review", "One remark not formalised at all"]}
note: "Our summary of CASRAI and lilting.ch, which quote the README and paper."
```

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **The claim:** computing the **permanent** of a matrix (outside characteristic 2) or the **Hamiltonian cycle polynomial** needs more than polynomially many steps, on every field.
- **The honest caveat:** in the README's words, "Lean checks the proofs, but **no machine checks that this file says what is claimed**." Per lilting.ch, the paper says that match was checked **only by another AI**. [(lilting.ch)](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/vp-vnp-valiant-conjecture-claude-lean)
- **CI passed:** the repo's automated check ran on **23 Nov** and succeeded.
- **Not a lone event:** it lands days after the Riemann and Navier-Stokes claims we covered in Issues #54 and #55.

**Why it matters.** If it holds, an AI has settled **a famous open problem in theoretical computer science**. Either way, **the bottleneck in AI maths is now human review**. Treat it as **a claim, not a result**, until complexity theorists sign off.

## Top story 2 · ShellBench: agents still fumble everyday life

<span class="tag dev">DEV</span><span class="tag watch">AGENTS</span>

**What happened.** A new benchmark tests AI agents on **real personal chores**: payments, schedules, family logistics. It's built from more than **10 million anonymised agent interactions** with The OpenClaw Foundation, Hugging Face, Red Hat, UC Berkeley and Parsewave. **Top models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta failed over a third of the time.** [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/even-the-best-ai-agents-struggle-with-everyday-life/)

```viz:bars
note: "Figures as reported by TechCrunch from ShellBench. 'Any safety rule' is across all models and tasks."
```

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Consistency is the problem:** each task ran **three times**. Top scorer **GPT-6 Astra** passed all three **less than half the time**.
- **Right answer, unsafe path:** about **10% of otherwise correct** attempts still broke a safety rule.
- **The harness matters:** Meta's Muse Wonder Full scored **56% in the Pi harness but 38.1% in Claude Code**. GPT-6 Astra beat Claude Fable 5.2 in Pi but lost in Claude Code.
- **Fair caveat:** the models weren't tested in their **own consumer apps** (Muse, Dots, Claude Duet), so results may differ there.

**Why it matters.** Every lab is selling **personal agents** right now. This is the first big test built from **real usage** rather than toy tasks, and it says **"supervised" is still the right setting**.

**🛠️ For devs**

Building an agent? **Run every eval task three times** and report pass-all-three, not best-of-one. Test **your harness, not just your model**: the same model swung 18 points between harnesses. And put **a confirm step in front of money and secrets**, always.
