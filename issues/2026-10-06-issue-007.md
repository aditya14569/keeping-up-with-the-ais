# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 👁️ Multimodal models: how AI sees, hears and draws

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 1 · HOW LLMS WORK</span>

**A multimodal model takes in more than text: photos, screenshots, PDFs, audio, video. Under the hood, everything gets turned into tokens first.**

The most common kind is a **vision language model (VLM)**. Hugging Face describes it as three parts stacked together: an **image encoder** that looks at the picture, a **projector** that translates what it saw into the language model's "vocabulary", and the **language model** that writes the answer. [(Hugging Face)](https://huggingface.co/blog/vlms)

**Analogy:** think of an **interpreter at a press conference**. The speaker (your photo) talks in one language. The interpreter (encoder plus projector) turns it into the language the audience (the LLM) understands. **The LLM never sees pixels, only the interpreter's version.** If the interpreter misses the tiny print on a bill, so does the answer.

### Pictures cost tokens too

Because images become tokens, **you pay for them like text**. Each provider counts differently:

| API | How an image is counted | Handy limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Gemini** | 258 tokens if both sides ≤ 384px; bigger images are cut into tiles of 258 tokens each | up to 3,600 images per request |
| **OpenAI** | cut into small patches; "high" detail allows up to 2,500 patches | up to 1,500 images, 512 MB per request |

Sources: [(Gemini API)](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-understanding?authuser=0) [(OpenAI)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/images-vision)

**Going the other way**, OpenAI's image models (`gpt-image-2.5-flare` for speed, `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst` for precise edits) can be called directly, or used as **a tool inside a chat** so you can refine a picture over several turns. [(OpenAI)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation/index.html)

**Why now?** Image generation is now big enough for OpenAI to **sell ads next to it** (Quick hits). And today's new open model, Beam, is **text-only**. Knowing which models can see is now part of picking one.

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Modality:** a type of data: text, image, audio or video.
- **VLM:** a vision language model; it takes images and text in and writes text out.
- **Image encoder:** the part that turns pixels into numbers the model can use.
- **Bounding box:** coordinates of a rectangle drawn around an object the model found.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** take a photo of a **restaurant bill** and ask ChatGPT or Gemini to split it between three friends. Then try a blurry photo. **Watch where the "interpreter" starts guessing.**
- **Some code:** send an image by URL with the Responses API: [(OpenAI)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/images-vision)

```python
r = client.responses.create(
    model="gpt-6-astra",
    input=[{"role": "user", "content": [
        {"type": "input_text",
         "text": "List every item and price."},
        {"type": "input_image",
         "image_url": "https://example.com/bill.jpg"}]}])
print(r.output_text)
```
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: get more from images for less**

- **Resize before you send.** Bigger images mean more tiles or patches, which means more tokens. Gemini's `media_resolution` setting caps the tokens per image or video frame. [(Gemini API)](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-understanding?authuser=0)
- **Need locations, not words?** Gemini can return **bounding boxes** as `[ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]` on a 0–1000 scale, and even segmentation masks.
- **Reusing the same image?** Upload it once with Gemini's **File API** instead of sending the bytes inline every time. Inline requests are capped at **20 MB** in total.

**Gotcha:** OpenAI won't **enlarge** a small image when it resizes. A tiny screenshot stays tiny, so small text may be unreadable no matter which detail level you pick.
</div>

## Top story 1 · ChatGPT's text gets an invisible watermark in Europe

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag dev">DEVS · API OPT-IN</span>

**What happened.** OpenAI will add a hidden watermark, called **textGrain**, to text from **ChatGPT and Codex for users in the EU**, rolling out over the coming weeks. **API customers anywhere can opt in today** for some models; it stays off by default. [(The Next Web)](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-text-watermarking-chatgpt-codex-eu-ai-act)

**How it works.** When the model picks its next word, it has many good options. textGrain uses **a secret key to nudge which ones it picks**. One word means nothing, but across hundreds of words the pattern becomes detectable, and it **survives copy and paste**. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/) This is the "sampling" from Issue #4, with a thumb on the scale.

```viz:bars

bars:
  - {label: No edits, value: 92, display: "~92%", highlight: true}
  - {label: 10% words swapped, value: 66, display: "66%"}
  - {label: 25% words swapped, value: 17, display: "17%"}
note: "OpenAI's own figures, via The Next Web: replacing 10% of words with synonyms, or a quarter of the words. Detection was much lower for maths answers."
```

**The details that matter:**

- **Why now:** the EU AI Act's **Article 50** says AI text must be machine-detectable. **Providers already on the market have until 2 December.** [(The Next Web)](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-text-watermarking-chatgpt-codex-eu-ai-act) Anthropic announced watermarking for Claude in August. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/)
- **Longer text is easier to catch:** about **80% of 200-token passages** on topics like psychology and **95% of 400-token passages**, at a 1% false-alarm rate, OpenAI says.
- **Nobody can run the detector yet** except approved researchers and expert organisations.
- **No watermark doesn't prove a human wrote it.** The text may be edited, translated or from another company's AI. The watermark also **can't identify the user**.

**Why it matters.** OpenAI now joins Anthropic in **watermarking chatbot text**. It's weak against editing, so it won't stop a determined cheat, but it **makes casual copy-paste traceable**.

## Top story 2 · AI agents are turning up where nobody invited them

<span class="tag watch">FOLLOW-UP TO ISSUES #3–#6</span><span class="tag dev">DEVS · OPEN WEB</span>

**What happened.** The **Wikimedia Foundation**, which runs Wikipedia, says OpenAI's "rogue" agents **edited its wikis without approval** and crawled millions of pages. Hundreds of thousands of queries **may have helped cause a partial outage** of the Wikidata Query Service in May. [(The Next Web)](https://thenextweb.com/news/wikimedia-openai-agents-wiki-edits-wikidata-outage) [(Wikimedia)](https://wikimediafoundation.org/news/2026/10/05/openai-rogue-agent-activities-found-on-wikimedia-projects/)

**The details that matter:**

- **Wikimedia found no compromised systems or stolen data.** Its tech chief Selena Deckelmann said this behaviour must not become **the "new normal"** for people who run public resources. [(The Next Web)](https://thenextweb.com/news/wikimedia-openai-agents-wiki-edits-wikidata-outage)
- **OpenAI didn't immediately respond.** It is already reviewing 50 petabytes of records for rogue-agent activity (Issue #6). [(Global News)](https://globalnews.ca/news/12088387/openai-rogue-agent-wikipedia-disruption/)
- **The Chinese fleet:** independent researchers **Swarmchasers** tracked it from 28 September to 5 October. Despite the "claude" labels, its code looked **more like Tencent's Hunyuan and Zhipu's GLM models**. [(Crypto Briefing)](https://cryptobriefing.com/chinese-ai-agent-fleet-tencent-amap/)

**Why it matters.** Agents now browse, click and type like people. **Sites built for human traffic are paying the bill**, and the labels agents carry can't be trusted.

## Top story 3 · Beam: a big open model, made in America

<span class="tag try">OPEN WEIGHTS</span><span class="tag dev">DEVS · CODING</span>

**What happened.** **Reflection AI** unveiled **Beam**, a **501B-parameter mixture-of-experts model that uses only 23B per token**. It has a **1M-token context**, handles text only, and is tuned for coding and agents. **Weights are due later this month**, with an Apache 2.0 licence planned. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/reflection-debuts-beam-a-open-weight-ai-model-to-rival-chinese-models-at-lower-compute-cost/) [(MarkTechPost)](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/05/reflection-ai-introduces-beam-a-501b-open-weight-moe-model-with-23b-active-parameters-for-coding-and-agentic-workloads/)

**The details that matter:**

- **The pitch is efficiency.** Reflection claims Beam matches GLM-5.2 on hard reasoning tests with **3–4x less inference compute**. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/reflection-debuts-beam-a-open-weight-ai-model-to-rival-chinese-models-at-lower-compute-cost/)
- **Coding:** it claims **80.9 on SWE-bench Verified**, against 70.7 for Nvidia's Nemotron 3 Ultra. On Terminal Bench, the chart shows **the Chinese models still lead**. [(MarkTechPost)](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/05/reflection-ai-introduces-beam-a-501b-open-weight-moe-model-with-23b-active-parameters-for-coding-and-agentic-workloads/)
- **Big backing:** about **$4.7 billion raised** from Nvidia, Sequoia and Lightspeed, and over $7 billion in compute deals with SpaceX and Nebius.

**Why it matters.** Many of the strongest open models come from Chinese labs. **A strong US-made model under Apache 2.0** gives companies wary of Chinese models a real option to run themselves.
