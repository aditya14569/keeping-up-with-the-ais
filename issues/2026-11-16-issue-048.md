# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🔬 Interpretability: looking inside a model

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 7 · NEWER TECHNIQUES</span>

**We built models that write code and pass exams. We still can't fully say how they do it.**

**The plain-English version.** A model is billions of numbers. When it reads your prompt, those numbers produce **activations**: a long list of values at every layer, like a snapshot of what it's "thinking". **Interpretability** is the science of turning those snapshots into ideas a human can name, such as "this is about Python" or "this sounds like a scam email".

**The catch:** you can't just read single neurons. Anthropic's researchers explain that **each concept is spread across many neurons, and each neuron helps with many concepts**. [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/mapping-mind-language-model) The usual explanation is **superposition**: models need to store **more ideas than they have neurons**, so they overlap them. [(Google PAIR)](https://pair.withgoogle.com/explorables/sae/)

**Analogy:** **a recording of a crowded party.** Each microphone picks up a dozen voices at once, so listening to one mic tells you little. A **sparse autoencoder** is the audio tool that separates the recording back into **individual speakers**. Each "speaker" it finds is a **feature**.

```viz:flow

columns:

note: "Simplified from Anthropic and Google PAIR's sparse-autoencoder explainer."
```

**How it works.** A sparse autoencoder (SAE) maps each activation into a **much bigger list where only a few entries light up**, then rebuilds the original from it. To name a feature, you collect the text that lights it up most and **ask an LLM for a label**. [(Google PAIR)](https://pair.withgoogle.com/explorables/sae/)

**The famous result.** In 2024 Anthropic pulled **millions of features** from the middle layer of **Claude 3 Sonnet**. One fired on the **Golden Gate Bridge**, in English, in Japanese, Chinese, Russian and more, and even on pictures of it. When they **turned that feature up**, Claude answered "what is your physical form?" by saying **it was the bridge**. [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/mapping-mind-language-model)

**Not just trivia.** Anthropic also found features for **scam emails, flattery, code backdoors and bias**. Dialling up the flattery feature made the model **heap untrue praise** on an overconfident user. [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/mapping-mind-language-model) Next step: **circuits**, which trace how features pass work to each other to reach an answer.

**Why now?** Labs watch a model's written-out reasoning to catch bad behaviour. Top story 2 says that window is **getting foggier**. Looking **inside the activations** is the backup plan.

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Activation:** the numbers a layer produces for your input.
- **Feature:** a direction in those numbers that matches one human idea.
- **Superposition:** many features crammed into fewer neurons, overlapping.
- **Steering:** adding a feature's direction to change the model's behaviour.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** open Google PAIR's **[sparse-autoencoder explorable](https://pair.withgoogle.com/explorables/sae/)**. Browse its map of **16,384 Gemma features** and try the steering demo.
- **Some code:** run the snippet below. It finds a "concept direction" in fake activations and steers with it.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: steering in 12 lines**

```python
import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(0); d = 64
bridge = rng.normal(size=d); bridge /= np.linalg.norm(bridge)
act = lambda b: rng.normal(size=d) + 3.0*b*bridge
on  = np.array([act(1) for _ in range(200)])
off = np.array([act(0) for _ in range(200)])
v = on.mean(0) - off.mean(0); v /= np.linalg.norm(v)
x = act(0); x2 = x + 4.0*v       # steer
# cosine(v, true feature): 0.97
# bridge score: 0.28 -> 4.28
```

This **"difference of means"** trick is the simplest way to find a direction. Real tools use SAEs on a real model's layers. Anthropic open-sourced **[circuit-tracer](https://www.anthropic.com/research/open-source-circuit-tracing)**, which draws **attribution graphs** for **Gemma-2-2b** and **Llama-3.2-1b**, with a browser view on Neuronpedia.

**Gotchas:** **labels are guesses**: check a feature's top examples yourself. **SAEs miss things**: Anthropic says its features are **a small subset** of what the model knows. **Steer gently**: push too hard and the model turns to mush.
</div>

## Top story 2 · OpenAI: models are getting harder to read

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag watch">SAFETY</span><span class="tag learn">RESEARCH</span>

**What happened.** OpenAI's alignment team published a study on how **chain-of-thought (CoT) monitorability** changes as models are trained. Their conclusion: **"as we scale toward ever-more intelligent systems, models are becoming harder to monitor through their CoT."** [(OpenAI)](https://alignment.openai.com/cot-monitorability-model-lifecycle)

**First, the idea.** Reasoning models "think out loud" before answering. Labs run **monitors** that read that thinking to catch cheating or sabotage. That only works if the bad idea **shows up in the writing**.

```viz:flow
title: "Two ways the monitor's view gets worse"
headers: [Training step, What grows, Effect]
columns:
  - [{label: Better pretraining, sub: "lower perplexity"}, {label: RL training, sub: "answer-only grading", style: soft}]
  - [{label: No-CoT skill, sub: "thinks without writing", style: accent}, {label: CoT control, sub: "can reshape its notes", style: accent}]
  - [{label: Less on the page, sub: "harder to monitor", style: warn}]
note: "Our simplification of OpenAI's two proxies for monitorability."
```

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **Two warning signs:** **no-CoT capability** (how much a model can reason in one step, with no written notes) and **CoT controllability** (how well it can change its notes on request).
- **Smarter means quieter:** no-CoT skill rose smoothly as base models got better, from **GPT-5 (Aug 2025)** to **Astra-class (Sep 2026)** models.
- **RL adds control:** controllability grew during reinforcement learning, even with **only final answers graded**. And a student model trained on a more controllable teacher's outputs **inherited it**.
- **Honest caveat:** OpenAI calls both measures **"imperfect proxies"**, and says it found GPT-6 Astra **couldn't evade full-context monitoring** of complex sabotage without guidance.

**Why it matters.** Reading the model's notes has been **the cheapest safety tool** labs have. If that window narrows, tools that read **activations directly** (Part 1) matter a lot more. OpenAI says further progress will **"require new approaches to monitorability."**

A separate Anthropic Fellows study this week found poisoned fine-tuning data can get **stronger** when mixed with clean data, raising a hidden misalignment rate **from 7% to 16%** in one test, so **audit training data as a whole**, not file by file. ## Top story 3 · OpenAI folds robotics into its main models

<span class="tag watch">ROBOTS</span><span class="tag dev">MODELS</span>

**What happened.** OpenAI confirmed to Fortune that it has **disbanded its stand-alone robotics team**. Researchers move under Chief Research Officer **Mark Chen**. Applied robotics and hardware staff move to President **Greg Brockman's** org. A spokesperson said it's meant to **speed robotics up**, not drop it. [(Fortune)](https://fortune.com/2026/11/13/openai-disbands-robotics-team-as-its-general-models-start-running-robots/)

<p style="break-after: avoid"><strong>The details that matter:</strong></p>

- **The bet:** OpenAI now thinks **general frontier models** like GPT-6 Astra can drive robots directly, without a separate robot "brain".
- **Still on the list:** a **humanoid robot demo in 2027**, a goal Sam Altman has set.
- **The doubts:** CMU's **Guanya Shi** read it as robotics becoming "no longer a special problem", while MIT's **Russ Tedrake** said robot companies still have an edge in **real-world data**.
- **The money:** Fortune notes OpenAI reportedly posted **$10.9B in operating losses** last quarter and has already dropped projects like **Sora**.

**Why it matters.** It's the same pattern we saw with coding and search: **one big general model eats a specialist field**. If it holds for robots, robot startups will compete on **hardware and data**, not on the model.
