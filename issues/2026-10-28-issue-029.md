# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## ⏱️ Latency: why your AI app feels slow (and how to fix it)

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 4 · SHIPPING FOR REAL</span>

**Users don't measure your model's IQ. They measure how long they stare at a blank box.**

**Latency** is the wait between sending a request and getting an answer. For LLMs it has two parts, and they have different causes and different fixes. [(Anthropic)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-latency)

- **Time to first token (TTFT):** how long before **the first word appears**. This is mostly the model reading your prompt.
- **Tokens per second:** how fast **the rest of the answer arrives** once it starts. This is the model writing.

**Analogy:** **a restaurant thali.** TTFT is how long before the first katori lands on your plate. Tokens per second is how quickly the rest keep coming. A kitchen that waits to serve everything at once feels slow even if it's fast. **Streaming is serving each dish as it's cooked.**

```viz:flow
title: "Where the time goes in one LLM request"
headers: [Send, Prefill, Decode, Done]
columns:
  - [{label: Network + queue, sub: "overhead", style: soft}]
  - [{label: Read the prompt, sub: "sets TTFT", style: accent}]
  - [{label: Write tokens, sub: "one at a time", style: accent}]
  - [{label: Full answer, sub: "total latency", style: soft}]
note: "Simplified. Prefill reads the whole prompt in one parallel pass; decode generates the answer one token at a time (Redis explainer)."
```

**The two phases.** In **prefill**, the model reads your whole prompt in one parallel pass, limited by **raw compute**. In **decode**, it writes one token at a time, each depending on the last, limited by **how fast memory can move data**. That's why long answers are slow even on huge GPUs. [(Redis)](https://redis.io/blog/prefill-vs-decode/)

**The rule of thumb that surprises people:** OpenAI says cutting **half your output tokens can cut about half the latency**, but cutting half your *input* tokens often saves only **1–5%**. **Shorter answers beat shorter prompts.** [(OpenAI)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latency-optimization)

```viz:versus
left: {emoji: 🐢, title: Wait for it all, points: ["Blank screen until **the last token**", "Feels slow even at 2 seconds", "Simple to code"], example: "response = client.create(...)"}
right: {emoji: ⚡, title: Stream it, points: ["First words in **about a second or less**", "User reads while it writes", "Handle events and errors mid-stream"], example: "for text in stream.text_stream: ..."}
note: "Streaming doesn't make the model faster; it makes the wait feel shorter. Examples are illustrative."
```

**The three levers:**

- **Stream** so people see text immediately. OpenAI calls it the most effective way to cut perceived waiting.
- **Pick a fast tier or smaller model** for easy jobs. Today's top story, GPT-6.1 Terra, is exactly that kind of model.
- **Generate less:** ask for short answers, use short JSON field names, set `max_tokens`.

**Why now?** Speed is the theme of the week. Apple claims M6 Macs reach **first token 1.2–1.3x faster** than M5 for local models [(Popular Science)](https://www.popsci.com/gear/apple-m6-chip-macbook-pro-imac-specs-details/), and Oracle's cloud lists **SambaNova chips that handle decode while GPUs do prefill**. [(The Next Platform)](https://www.nextplatform.com/ai/2026/10/27/oracle-is-expanding-the-accelerator-options-in-oci-for-enterprises/5305815)

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **TTFT:** time to first token. The **"is it thinking?" pause** before text appears.
- **Tokens per second:** output speed once the answer starts. Sets how long **long answers** take.
- **Fast tier:** the same or a similar model served on **priority hardware for a higher price**.
- **Small model:** fewer parameters, **faster and cheaper**, good enough for routine jobs like sorting or summarising.
</div>

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** ask your chatbot the same question in its **instant mode** and its **thinking mode**. Use your phone's stopwatch twice: **time to first word**, and **time to last word**. Then add "answer in one sentence" and time it again.
- **Some code:** run the script below with a **small model and a big one**, and with and without "be brief". Write down TTFT and total time. **You now have a latency budget.**
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: stream, and measure TTFT yourself**

```python
import time, anthropic
client = anthropic.Anthropic()
t0, first = time.perf_counter(), None
with client.messages.stream(model="claude-haiku-5-5", max_tokens=300,
        messages=[{"role": "user", "content": "Explain TTFT in 2 lines"}]) as s:
    for text in s.text_stream:
        first = first or time.perf_counter() - t0
        print(text, end="", flush=True)
print(f"\nTTFT {first:.2f}s · total {time.perf_counter() - t0:.2f}s")
```

**Gotchas:** errors like `overloaded_error` can arrive **mid-stream**, so handle them inside the loop, not just at HTTP level. `usage` in a stream is a **running total**. Tool inputs arrive as **partial JSON**, so parse only after the block ends. A hard `max_tokens` cap can stop **mid-word**. [(Anthropic)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/claude/reference/messages-streaming)
</div>

| Symptom | Likely cause | First fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Long pause, then text** | Big prompt or busy model (prefill) | Stream; trim context; smaller model |
| **Text starts fast, crawls** | Long answer (decode) | Ask for less; set `max_tokens` |
| **Slow every time, even short** | Too many calls in a row | Merge steps; run calls in parallel |
| **Slow only at peak hours** | Queueing on shared capacity | Fast or priority tier for live users |

## Top story 1 · GPT-6.1 Terra: OpenAI's fast middle model

<span class="tag hot">NEW MODEL</span><span class="tag try">TRY IT</span><span class="tag learn">LATENCY</span>

**What happened.** On Monday, OpenAI released **GPT-6.1 Terra**, the middle of its four-model lineup (Luna, Terra, Sol, Astra). It's built for **everyday computer use**: reading screens, clicking through apps, drafting replies. It's in ChatGPT Work and Codex for **Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu**, and in the API as `gpt-6.1-terra`. Free users stay on GPT-5.6 Luna. [(Decrypt)](https://decrypt.co/381883/openai-launches-gpt-6-1-terra-half-good-half-price)

**The details that matter:**

- **Price:** **$1 input / $5 output** per million tokens, and cached input at **$0.05**. [(Artificial Analysis)](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/gpt-6-1-terra-pushes-the-cost-efficiency-frontier)
- **Speed:** OpenAI claims Terra finishes tasks **more than 3x faster on average** than GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Astra.
- **Quality:** Artificial Analysis puts it **4 points below Sol** on its intelligence index and scores it **48 vs Sol's 54** on coding agents. Its hallucination rate fell to **38% from 88%**.
- **Fewer errors (OpenAI's own test):** at low effort, the error rate on tricky prompts dropped from **53.6% to 16.7%**. [(9to5Mac)](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/26/openai-upgrades-chatgpt-and-codex-with-new-gpt-6-1-terra-model/)
- **Context:** OpenRouter lists **1.1M tokens**, plus a cheaper **Flex** option at half price. [(OpenRouter)](https://openrouter.ai/openai/gpt-6.1-terra)

**Why it matters.** This is Part 1 in action. Most agent steps (read a page, click a button, fill a form) don't need the smartest model. They need **a good-enough model that answers fast**. For many apps, routing those steps to Terra and **saving Sol for the hard parts** cuts both cost and wait time.

## Top story 2 · Update: Claude says it disproved the same conjecture

<span class="tag watch">RESEARCH</span><span class="tag learn">AI FOR SCIENCE</span><span class="tag watch">CLAIMS</span>

**What happened.** Hours after OpenAI claimed its model had disproved the **Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH)** (Issue #28), Anthropic posted its own paper, credited to **"Claude (Anthropic)"**. Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says Claude found the key idea on **16 October**, using **14 million output tokens** with no human help on the discovery. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/frontier-ai-labs-say-their-models-refuted-the-exponential-time-hypothesis-what-does-that-mean/)

**The details that matter:**

- **What it claims:** a 3-SAT algorithm that beats brute force by more than ETH allows. It also claims to break **SETH**, a stronger version, and gives faster theoretical algorithms for problems like Clique and Vertex Cover. [(Tech Times)](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329569/20261027/two-ai-systems-disproved-same-complexity-conjecture-independently-same-day-800-papers-risk.htm)
- **Checked by software:** the proof passes Lean 4 and a second, independent checker. But Anthropic says **no human had checked the Lean code by hand** when it was published.
- **Same answer twice:** mathematician Jelani Nelson, who read Claude's proof, says both labs reach **roughly the same bound**.
- **Not practical:** it only beats brute force at around **10^10,000 variables**.
- **The reviewers are careful:** Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams say their involvement **"should not be taken as certification."** Anthropic counts about **800 papers** that rely on ETH.

**Why it matters.** Two labs reaching the same result separately makes it more likely to be real, but **it still needs human review**. The lesson for builders: **long autonomous runs plus a strict checker** now produce work experts take seriously.

It needs **macOS 27** and arrives in **November**. The apps also get a beta assistant built on **Apple's models plus OpenAI's**, for Creator Studio subscribers ($12.99/month).
