# Part 1 · Learn one thing

## 🧩 Tokens: how models read text (and why it affects your bill)

<span class="tag learn">4 MIN READ</span><span class="tag learn">MODULE 1 · HOW LLMS WORK</span>

**Models don't read words. They read tokens, and you pay per token.**

Before a model sees your prompt, a small program called a **tokenizer** chops the text into chunks. A chunk might be a whole common word ("the"), part of a word ("token" + "ization"), a space plus a word, or a single punctuation mark. Each chunk gets an ID number from a fixed list. **The model only ever sees those numbers**, never letters.

```viz:flow
title: What happens to your prompt
headers: [You type, Tokenizer, Model sees, You pay for]
columns:
  - - label: "\"Unbelievable!\""
      sub: 1 word, 13 letters
  - - label: Split into chunks
      sub: "Un · believ · able · !"
      style: soft
  - - label: Token IDs
      sub: a short list of numbers
      style: accent
    - label: Writes new IDs
      sub: one token at a time
      style: accent
  - - label: Input tokens
      sub: everything you send
      style: warn
    - label: Output tokens
      sub: everything it writes
      style: warn
note: The split shown is an illustration. Every model family has its own tokenizer, so the same text can become a different number of tokens.
```

**Analogy:** think of a **phone data plan**. You don't pay per website, you pay per megabyte. AI APIs work the same way. You don't pay per question, you pay per token, both for what you **send** (input) and what comes **back** (output). A short question with a 50-page PDF attached is a "heavy" request, even if the answer is one line.

### The rules of thumb

OpenAI's own guide says that in English, **one token is about 4 characters, or about ¾ of a word**. So 100 tokens is roughly 75 words. [(OpenAI Help Center)](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/4936856-what-are-tokens-and-how-to-count-them) The same guide warns that **other languages split differently**. Hindi, Tamil or Hinglish text can come out quite differently, so test your own text instead of assuming.

| Thing | Roughly | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 token | ~4 English characters | The unit everything is priced in |
| 1 page of text (500 words) | ~670 tokens | Handy for sizing documents |
| Input tokens | Cheaper | Your prompt, files, chat history |
| Output tokens | Usually **5× pricier** today | What the model writes back |
| Cached input | Up to **95% off** | Repeated context you resend |

The page estimate is our maths from OpenAI's ¾-word rule. The 5× and 95% figures come from this week's GPT-6.1 Sol and Gemini 4 Argon price lists (see Part 2).

<div class="box jargon" markdown="1">
**📖 Jargon buster**

- **Tokenizer:** the program that turns text into token IDs and back. Each model family has its own.
- **Input vs output tokens:** what you send vs what the model writes. They're priced separately.
- **Per million tokens (/M):** how prices are quoted. "$2/M input" means $2 for every million tokens you send.
- **Cached input:** context the provider has seen recently (like a long system prompt). It's billed at a big discount.
</div>

### Why now?

This week two top labs priced new models at exactly **$2/M in and $10/M out**, and Google raised its output limit to **a million tokens**. When models can write a novel-length answer in one go, knowing what a token costs stops being trivia. **It decides whether your side project costs pocket money or a lot more.**

<div class="box tryit" markdown="1">
**🧪 Try it in 10 minutes**

- **No code:** open OpenAI's free [Tokenizer tool](https://platform.openai.com/tokenizer). Paste an English paragraph, then the same idea in Hindi or Hinglish, then a chunk of JSON. Compare the counts and watch where it splits words.
- **Some code:** count the tokens in your own app's system prompt (snippet below). Multiply by your daily requests. That's your fixed cost before anyone types anything.
</div>

<div class="box devs" markdown="1">
**🛠️ For devs: count before you send**

```python
import anthropic
client = anthropic.Anthropic()
r = client.messages.count_tokens(
    model="claude-opus-5-5",
    system=SYSTEM_PROMPT,
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": doc_text}],
)
print(r.input_tokens)   # free endpoint, own rate limit
```

Anthropic's `count_tokens` endpoint is **free** and has its own rate limit. [(Claude Docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/token-counting) For OpenAI models, use the `tiktoken` library. **The bill-math:** `cost = in_tokens × in_price + out_tokens × out_price`. For example, a request with 2,000 tokens in and 500 out on GPT-6.1 Sol costs $0.004 + $0.005, so about **$0.009**. **Gotchas:** chat history is resent on *every* turn, so input grows each message. Reasoning models bill their hidden "thinking" as output. And watch for price cliffs: Sol charges **2× input and 1.5× output on the whole request** once input passes 272K tokens. [(SitePoint)](https://www.sitepoint.com/gpt-6-1-sol-developers-pricing-quickstart/)
</div>

### Tokens explain some weird AI habits

Because the model sees chunks, not letters, a few famous failures suddenly make sense. Andrej Karpathy's tokenizer lecture traces many of them back to tokenization. [(fast.ai)](https://www.fast.ai/posts/2025-10-16-karpathy-tokenizers)

```viz:versus
title: What the model "sees" changes what it's good at
left:
  emoji: 🔤
  title: Hard for models
  points:
    - Counting letters in a word (it sees chunks, not letters)
    - Reversing or spelling words letter by letter
    - Long-digit arithmetic, when numbers split oddly
right:
  emoji: 💡
  title: What helps
  points:
    - Ask it to **spell the word out** first, then count
    - Let it **use a tool** (code) for maths
    - Keep prompts lean, since every token is paid for
note: General patterns, not guarantees. Newer reasoning models handle many of these better.
```

## Top story 1 · Google's Gemini 4 Argon takes the benchmark lead, but you can't use it yet

<span class="tag hot">BIG DEAL</span><span class="tag watch">LIMITED RELEASE</span>

**What happened.** On 30 September Google announced **Gemini 4 Argon**, its new frontier model, nearly a year after Gemini 3. It's built for long, complex work in coding, finance, law and cybersecurity. [(Axios)](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/30/google-gemini-4) For now it's going only to **cybersecurity partners** in Google's Fairwind Program and to US government pre-release testing. [(TechCrunch)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/google-releases-gemini-4-argon-called-its-most-powerful-model-yet/)

**The details that matter:**

- **Benchmarks:** Google published 18 results. **Argon leads outright on 12**, GPT-6 Astra on 3 and Claude Opus 5.5 on 2. Argon trails on some agentic coding tests, for example Terminal-bench 4.0, where it scores 57.4% vs Opus 5.5's 66.4%. [(VentureBeat)](https://venturebeat.com/technology/google-unveils-gemini-4-argon-retaking-benchmark-lead-over-openai-and-anthropic-but-in-limited-release)
- **Price:** $2/M input and $10/M output during launch, with **95% off cached input**. After that it rises to $4/$20. [(9to5Google)](https://9to5google.com/2026/09/30/gemini-4-argon-announcement/)
- **Long answers:** the output limit jumps to **1 million tokens**, up from 64,000. [(SiliconANGLE)](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/30/googles-new-frontier-ai-model-gemini-4-argon-goes-to-cybersecurity-defenders-first/)
- **Who's next:** Google AI Ultra subscribers and paid API customers, but **no date yet**. [(SiliconANGLE)](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/30/googles-new-frontier-ai-model-gemini-4-argon-goes-to-cybersecurity-defenders-first/)

**Why it matters.** Google says Argon can **find, check and patch serious security bugs on its own**, which is why defenders get it first. It also claims Argon converted 32,000 lines of video-decoder code to safe Rust. [(SiliconANGLE)](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/30/googles-new-frontier-ai-model-gemini-4-argon-goes-to-cybersecurity-defenders-first/) A model that powerful at finding bugs is useful to attackers too. **"Defenders first, everyone later"** is quickly becoming the standard way to launch a frontier model.

## Top story 2 · Update: GPT-6.1 Sol, near-flagship brains at a fifth of the price

<span class="tag try">AVAILABLE NOW</span><span class="tag dev">API · CODEX · COPILOT</span>

**What happened.** Yesterday we flagged **GPT-6.1 Sol** from DevDay as one to come back to. OpenAI pitches it as "near-Astra intelligence for a fifth of the price." It costs **$2/M input, $10/M output**, and $0.10/M for cached input. It's live in the API (`gpt-6.1-sol`), Codex and ChatGPT Work, but not yet in regular ChatGPT chat. [(The Next Web)](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-gpt-6-1-sol-price-astra-devday) It's also rolling out in **GitHub Copilot** for Pro+, Max, Business and Enterprise users. [(SitePoint)](https://www.sitepoint.com/gpt-6-1-sol-developers-pricing-quickstart/)

**The details that matter:**

- **Context:** a 1.05M-token context window and up to 128K output tokens. [(OpenRouter)](https://openrouter.ai/openai/gpt-6.1-sol)
- **Effort dial:** reasoning effort runs from **low to max**. There's no "none" or "minimal" setting. [(SitePoint)](https://www.sitepoint.com/gpt-6-1-sol-developers-pricing-quickstart/)
- **Cost per task:** OpenAI says it averaged **$5.47 per task** on Terminal-Bench Science, vs about $23 for Opus 5.5 and Astra. Astra still scored higher on that test. [(The Next Web)](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-gpt-6-1-sol-price-astra-devday)
- **Fewer mistakes:** OpenAI says Sol's factual error rate is 7.7%, down from 11.4%. These are **OpenAI's own numbers**. [(The Next Web)](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-gpt-6-1-sol-price-astra-devday)

**Why it matters.** For most apps the question isn't "which model is smartest?" but **"which is good enough per rupee?"** Two labs landing on the same $2/$10 price in one week looks like the new going rate for a strong workhorse model.

## Top story 3 · Washington renames AI "Super Intelligence" and gets a safety pledge

<span class="tag watch">POLICY</span><span class="tag hot">NO TEETH (YET)</span>

**What happened.** Yesterday we asked how the 29 September White House meeting would go. Here's the answer. President Trump signed an **executive order telling federal agencies to replace "artificial intelligence" with "Super Intelligence" (SI)** in official documents and websites. At the same summit, tech leaders signed a **White House Accord on Super Intelligence**, which Trump called "morally binding." [(Fox Business)](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/trump-signs-executive-order-rebranding-ai-super-intelligence-tech-titans-ink-separate-accord)

**The details that matter.** The six signatories were Sundar Pichai, Dario Amodei, Mark Zuckerberg, Greg Brockman, Elon Musk and Jensen Huang. They committed to monitor model risks, including **cyber, bio and chemical threats**, and to work with independent auditors. [(Analytics Insight)](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/news/trump-tech-ceos-meeting-ai-renamed-super-intelligence-safety-pact-more) Reuters noted the accord has **no penalties, no mandatory disclosures and no deadline**. [(Tech Startups, citing Reuters)](https://techstartups.com/2026/09/30/top-tech-news-today-september-30-2026-deepseek-anthropic-google-meta-openai-robinhood-more/)

**Why it matters.** For builders in India, nothing changes on Monday. But the accord's focus on cyber risk matches the Argon launch and Anthropic's GLM-5.3 report (below). **Cybersecurity is where AI policy is getting serious first.**
